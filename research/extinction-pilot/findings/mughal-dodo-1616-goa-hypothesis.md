# Hypothesis: the Mughal dodo came from the 1616 Jesuit voyage (Goa → Mauritius → Goa)

Working note, 1 Oct 2026. Not yet a claim. Sources read directly unless marked.

## Hypothesis

The live dodo painted at Jahangir's court, the St Petersburg Muraqqa "Page of Birds" attributed to Ustad Mansur, came from the 1616 Portuguese Jesuit expedition. That expedition caught a young dodo on Mauritius in March 1616 and returned to Goa. The bird then reached the court through the Goa rarity trade. On this reading the painting dates to c. 1616–20, not c. 1625–28.

## Evidence for

1. **A young dodo was caught by a Goa expedition.** Manuel de Almeida SJ's report of 25 May 1616 (French translation in Grandidier et al., *Collection des ouvrages anciens concernant Madagascar*, II, 1904, p. 117) reads: "Nous avons pris dans cette île une autruche toute jeune encore, quoiqu'elle fût déjà plus grosse qu'un dindon ordinaire" (We caught on this island a very young ostrich, though it was already bigger than an ordinary turkey). Grandidier glosses it "[un Dronte ou Didus ineptus]".
   - Mauritius had no ratites, and a bird "bigger than a turkey" there can only be a dodo. Cheke and Parish 2020 (Table A1, note 4) also identify Almeida's bird as the dodo. Their wording, that Almeida "described the Dodo as like a young ostrich", treats the passage as a description, not a capture.
   - Faria y Sousa's printed account of the same voyage (*Ásia Portuguesa*; Grandidier II, p. 274) lists "autruches" among the island's fauna but mentions no capture.
2. **The ships returned to Goa.** The two vessels, a caravel and a patache under capitão-mor Pero d'Almeida Cabral, left Goa on 1 Feb 1616 and sighted Cirne on 20 March. They stayed four days and reached south-east Madagascar in April.
   - The patache, under João Cardoso de Pina, left south-east Madagascar on 31 May 1616 and **anchored at Goa on 1 Sept 1616** (Grandidier II, pp. 207–208). It carried a Malagasy hostage-prince, who was presented to Viceroy D. Jerónimo de Azevedo.
   - The caravel stayed to explore the east coast. Cordara (Grandidier II, p. 272) says the ships brought the Fathers back to Goa within the year.
   - The Viceroy's orders were to survey Cirne and Mascarenhas and "fizessem particular relação de tudo o que achassem" (make a particular report of everything they found; Vicente 2015, p. 28). That is an exploring brief.
3. **Survival is plausible.** A juvenile caught in March 1616 would be adult by about 1617–18. Large pigeons are long-lived in captivity; dodo lifespan is estimated at 15–20+ years. The London dodo (1638) and the Japan dodo (1647) show live dodos travelled on long voyages.
4. **The painting's date is not secure.**
   - Salim Ali (in Alvi & Rahman, *Jahangir the Naturalist*, 1968) says the miniature is "unsigned and undated". He places it in 1624–27 only because Jahangir's memoirs, which end in 1624, do not mention a dodo. He links it to the Surat dodos Mundy saw in 1628, after Jahangir's death (Oct 1627), which is internally inconsistent.
   - **The holding institution, the Institute of Oriental Manuscripts RAS, dates the dodo study "circa 1615"** (Google Arts & Culture entry, "A Page of Birds. St. Petersburg Muraqqa"). The page is a collage, with backgrounds added in Isfahan; the album came through Nadir Shah's 1739 loot.
   - Other sources give c. 1610, c. 1615, c. 1623 or c. 1625.
5. **The channel existed.** Muqarrab Khan bought rarities for Jahangir at Goa, including a turkey in 1612 (Rogers & Beveridge, *Tuzuk* I, pp. 215–216). He was governor of the Gujarat ports in these years.

## Evidence against, or gaps

- **"Pris" may not mean kept alive.** "Pris" (taken) could mean caught and eaten. The text survives only in French translation.
  - The Portuguese originals (the Conde do Ameal's Madagascar codex, Coimbra) were sold at auction after Grandidier's copyist used them. Their current location is unknown (Vicente 2015, Anexo VI note).
- **No arrival record.** The account of the patache's arrival at Goa describes only the hostage's presentation, with no bird. The caravel's return is not described.
- **Jahangir's silence.** His memoirs are detailed for 1616–22 and record rare animals (turkey 1612; Abyssinian elephant; zebra 1621), but never a dodo.
  - At Ahmedabad in early 1618 he wrote that Muqarrab Khan's presents held "nothing rare". This counter-evidence is real, but the memoirs also omit many animals that were painted.
- **Portuguese–Mughal disruption.** Relations were broken in 1613–15 after the Rahimi seizure. Roe reports the Goa "caffila" (convoy) failed in June 1617.
- **Attribution and dating rest on style.** Iwanow 1958 and Stresemann 1958 (*J. Ornithol.* 99) and the album catalogue need reading for the dating rationale.
- **Competing route.** The English factory at Surat held two live dodos in 1628 (Mundy). They are plausibly from Captain Hall's fleet, which lay at Mauritius 28 May–18 June 1628 and returned to Swally (Surat's anchorage) on 30 Sept 1628. That was after Jahangir's death, so it fits only if the painting is post-1627, i.e. Shah Jahan's reign.

## Parish 2013 checked (full text, 1 Oct 2026)

- **Pp. 30–31, Almeida.** Parish quotes the Grandidier translation ("We took in this island an ostrich still very young…") as a dodo record. He notes that Hume (in Cheke & Hume 2008) thought it "might possibly represent a juvenile dodo".
  - He cites a Portuguese text of an anonymous version, "Da segunda Missão que fizerão os Padres da Compagnia de Jesus a Ilha de São Lourenço no anno de 1616", printed in **Leitão 1970**, *Os dois descobrimentos da ilha de São Lourenço* (Lisbon, CEHU), p. 280. It reads "We met emas, cranes, parrots…"; per Leitão, the manuscript is probably an 18th-century copy.
  - He does **not** discuss whether the bird was kept alive or its return to Goa.
- **Pp. 72–74, the Mughal painting.**
  - The image is in the St Petersburg Muraqqa (E 14, fol. 80r). It is unsigned and attributed to Mansur on technical and stylistic grounds, and was pasted into a collage compiled by Muhammad Baqir.
  - Stresemann (1958) put it at 1624 at the earliest; Das (1973) at 1624–27, because the *Tuzuk* is silent. Parish also notes datings of c. 1615 (Ziswiler 1996) to c. 1625.
  - Parish: the dodo was "probably a gift … from the English or the Dutch, or possibly the Portuguese". There is no evidence of a Surat menagerie, and a link to Mundy's birds is "very improbable" unless the bird belonged to Shah Jahan.
- **What this leaves as new.** The timing problem and the possibility of a Portuguese gift are already in Parish. Not in Parish, Cheke & Parish 2020 or Salim Ali: **the specific link between Almeida's March 1616 capture of a juvenile by a Goa-based expedition (patache at Goa 1 Sept 1616) and the Mughal painting**, and the use of that link to support an earlier (c. 1616–20) date.
- **Portuguese text.** The Portuguese wording of the capture sentence ("tomámos"? "apanhámos"?) has not been seen. Check whether Leitão 1970 prints Almeida's own report as well as the anonymous version.

## Corrections and checks (1 Oct 2026, after GPT-6 review)

- **Dating.** The c. 1615 date is S. C. Welch's art-historical dating in the 1996 *St Petersburg Muraqqa* catalogue, repeated by Ebba Koch in 2009. It is not an independent institutional finding.
- **Two dating traditions.** Art historians put the picture at c. 1615; the natural-history literature puts it at 1624–27.
  - **Stresemann 1958 (read directly)** identifies the loriculus (*Tuzuk*, 1614) and the tragopan (*Tuzuk*, 1621) on the same collage page. He then argues that because the dodo is absent from the memoirs, which end in 1624, it was painted "frühestens 1624, spätestens 1627" (1624 at the earliest, 1627 at the latest). This is purely an argument from silence.
  - He thinks one of Mundy's Surat birds is "nicht ausgeschlossen" (not ruled out). He does not mention Almeida or Goa.
- **Parish p. 30's "departed May 26" is a slip.** Grandidier's text gives a four-day stay from 20–21 March 1616 (the report itself is dated 25 May).
- **Manuscript.** Almeida's report **was** in the Ameal codex: Grandidier II, p. 106 note, lists the codex's fascicle 7 as "Rapport du P. Man. d'Almeida du 25 mai 1616". Per the Persée review of Leitão 1970, the 1616 *relação* original was lost by 1970, so Leitão **back-translated Grandidier's French into Portuguese**.
  - The only Portuguese witness is the anonymous "Da segunda Missão…" (probably an 18th-century copy), printed by Leitão (p. 280 for the fauna list with "emas"). Its holding library still needs identifying from Leitão.
- **Which ship.**
  - The Superior's instructions put Almeida (with Custódio da Costa and Dom André) on the **caravel**, and Mariano and Azevedo on the **patache**.
  - Almeida stayed ashore in Anosy (south-east Madagascar) through at least July 1616, then went to Sahadia and Mozambique (letters of 1617).
  - "Nous avons pris" is a fleet-level "we". Which vessel held the bird, and whether the caravel returned to Goa in 1616, is **unresolved**.
  - The patache's arrival at Goa on 1 Sept 1616 does not by itself close the chain.
- **Capture is not transport.** Alive, kept, and shipped are all unproven.

## A2/A3 follow-up (1 Oct 2026)

- **A second Goa–Mauritius–Goa voyage, 1617.** Per Faria y Sousa (Grandidier II, pp. 275–278), Manoel Freyre de Andrade's ship left Goa in early Feb 1617 with Andriantsambatra ("D. Jeronimo"), gifts and two Jesuits (Paulo Jovio, Pedro Garreiro).
  - It watered at Cirne, raided the Dutch salvage camp (three wrecks, about twenty Dutchmen) and went on to Madagascar.
  - It brought D. Jeronimo and a captured prince, Andriamahasoa, back to Goa, where the prince was baptised and died.
  - Bocarro's *Década 13* (ch. CLXVI) records the raid but no fauna. This is a second opportunity for a dodo to reach Goa (1617).
- **Caravel of 1616.** Its route after Anosy (June 1616) is still not established in the Grandidier texts. Faria has the capitão-mor Pedro de Almeida Cabral bring Andriantsambatra to Goa; the Cardoso de Pina narrative has the patache bring him. Faria swaps the captains' names elsewhere.
- **Ameal codex (A3).**
  - The Ameal library was auctioned in Lisbon, 1–16 April 1924 (Palácio Azambuja, Largo do Calhariz 17). Catalogue: *Catálogo da notável e preciosa livraria que foi do ilustre bibliófilo conimbricense Conde do Ameal* (José dos Santos, preface by Gustavo de Matos Sequeira; Porto, 1924; about 770 pp.). It is not online, but copies circulate in the antiquarian trade and are at BNP.
  - **Pedro Pinto**, the palaeographer of Vicente 2015, wrote on geneall.net (19 Jul 2011) that he had sought for years "um códice com cartas sobre a Ilha de São Lourenço … no século XVII, compradas pelo Matos Sequeira" (a codex of letters about São Lourenço in the 17th century, bought by Matos Sequeira). The family no longer has it; some of Matos Sequeira's papers may have gone to the Grupo/Gabinete de Estudos Olisiponenses (Lisbon).
  - Leads: the catalogue's lot description and buyer list; Gabinete de Estudos Olisiponenses holdings; contacting Pedro Pinto (CHAM/NOVA) about any progress since 2011.
- **The Viceroy's own report on the 1616 voyage's return** should be in his outgoing letters of late 1616 or early 1617. Candidates: Historical Archives of Goa, Livros das Monções liv. 12–13 (some printed in the *Boletim Official do Estado da India*, 1883); Torre do Tombo, Documentos Remetidos da Índia liv. 9–10 (images on digitarq). Leitão 1970 likely gathers these.

## 8 Oct 2026: Cambay 1617 and the GPT-6 review

- **Jahangir at Cambay, Dec 1617 (Tuzuk I, Rogers & Beveridge pp. 415–419).**
  - The emperor camped about ten days "on the shore of the salt sea" at Cambay, in the 12th regnal year. He left on 19 Dey, which is c. 30 Dec 1617. Beveridge's bracket gives "1618", which is a slip.
  - Muqarrab Khan met him on the road with a pearl.
  - "Some ghurabs from European ports", meaning Portuguese cafila craft, were "decorated" and "showed" to him on the 10th before they sailed. On the 11th he went aboard a ghurab himself.
  - This is the only time in the reign that the emperor was in person at a Portuguese-frequented port. It falls about 15 months after the 1616 patache reached Goa (1 Sept 1616) and within months of the 1617 Freire de Andrade voyage (Goa–Cirne–Madagascar–Goa).
  - A Goa-held dodo has its best documented occasion to reach Jahangir here, Dec 1617 – early 1618, via Cambay or Muqarrab Khan at Ahmadabad.
  - Unverified: the Persian text of the ghurab passage. Check whether "European" renders *Firangi* and whether anything shown is itemised (Persian text, Aligarh 1864 ed.).
- **Prediction.** If the dodo came by this route, the Mansur study belongs c. 1617–19. That is compatible with Welch's c. 1615 stylistic date and with Stresemann's identification of the loriculus (1614) on the same collage page. It does not need the bird to live until 1624.
- **Ship conflict.** Mariano writes on 18 June that "la patache va partir", while Cardoso de Pina has the patache leaving on 31 May. Either one date is wrong or a second small vessel (the rebuilt barque?) sailed in June. Unresolved.
- **Almeida's report rode the patache.** It is dated 25 May, six days before the patache sailed for Goa. The passage reporting the bird was therefore read at Goa from Sept 1616, whatever became of the bird.
- **The 1612 Goa purchase already included Madagascar fauna.** The "monkey" with a fox's face, hawk's eyes, ash-coloured woolly fur and wine-red ear-to-chin patch (Tuzuk I p. 216) reads as a lemur. Goa's rarity trade was supplying western Indian Ocean island animals to the court before the 1616 voyage.

## 8 Oct 2026 (second pass): local checks

- **Faria y Sousa, Spanish original** (*Asia Portuguesa* III, pt. 2, cap. XIV, pp. 317–319; archive.org `res-754-a_0000_capa-capa_t24-c-r0150_202602`).
  - The fauna of "la Isla del Cisne" includes "avestruzes, grullas, papagayos, garças" (ostriches, cranes, parrots, herons). There is **no capture**.
  - The 1617 voyage: Manuel Freyre de Andrade's patache left Goa "al principio de Febrero" (early February). It watered at Cisne, burned the Dutch salvage camp and took prisoners, then went to S. Lucas. It brought back to Goa the Jesuits (Almeida, Costa, Azevedo, Mariano) and a captured prince, Anrias Massa, later Dom Francisco Xavier. Return date not given; at the latest late 1617.
- **Two Portuguese texts behind the capture sentence.**
  - Grandidier II p. 106 says the anonymous *Da segunda Missão* (Ameal codex) narrates the Goa–Mauritius–Madagascar voyage "en termes identiques" to Almeida's report, "avec un peu moins de détails" (in identical terms, with slightly less detail).
  - Parish p. 30 translates Leitão 1970 p. 280: "We met emas, cranes, parrots, herons, thrushes, galeirões…". That bird list differs from Grandidier's French (parrots, cranes, herons, merles, ducks). It has *galeirões* and puts emas first, so Leitão p. 280 is a genuine Portuguese text, not a back-translation.
  - The capture sentence ("pris … toute jeune") is therefore one of Almeida's extra details, attested only in French. **Leitão p. 280 must be read to see whether *Da segunda Missão* has a verb like "tomámos".**
- **The age fits the biology.** Angst, Chinsamy, Steel & Hume 2017 (*Sci. Rep.* 7:7993, PMC5570941), from bone histology:
  - dodos bred around August;
  - chicks "grew quickly to almost adult body size" before the November–March cyclone season.
  - A bird hatched c. Aug–Sept 1615 would be about 6 months old on 21–24 March 1616: "toute jeune encore" yet "plus grosse qu'un dindon".
  - Almeida's description is biologically exact for the season, which argues against reading it as an adult mistaken for a juvenile.
- **Independent Dutch witness to the 1616 stop** (probably unused in the dodo literature).
  - Source: J. P. Coen, Bantam, to the Amsterdam chamber, written after 26 Oct 1616 (NA 1.04.02 inv. 1065, scan 0089, GLOBALISE HTR).
  - Five days after the *Witte Beer* left Mauritius, "2 portugesche scheepkens yder van ontrent 70 lasten" (two small Portuguese ships of about 70 lasts each) arrived "van Goa", each with about 80 men, laden with lime and stone for a fort at "Sant Lissia" (Santa Luzia), and carrying the Malagasy king's son.
  - They seized two Dutchmen. The Dutchmen escaped by giving the slip to two of their three guards, "die … met hun waeren gegaen om verckens te vanghen" (who had gone with them to catch pigs). So the Portuguese were **hunting live game inland with Dutch guides**, and the context of the capture is attested independently.
  - Ship size: about 70 lasts is roughly 140 tons each.
- **Shah Jahan's Goa channel, 1617.**
  - At Mandu, Oct 1617, Prince Khurram's offerings included a ruby "bought for my son at the port of Goa for 200,000 rupees" (Tuzuk I, p. 399).
  - Two Mughal purchasing channels at Goa were therefore active in 1616–17: Muqarrab Khan's, and the prince's Deccan agents.
- **Counter-evidence, verbatim** (Tuzuk I, p. 435; 4 Isfandarmuz, c. mid-Feb 1618, leaving Ahmadabad): Muqarrab Khan's presents held "nothing rare among them, nor anything that I took a fancy to". The most direct Mughal channel shows no rarity in Feb 1618.
- **The other birds on fol. 80r** (Parish pp. 72–74, after Stresemann and Den Hengst):
  - painted sandgrouse, western tragopan, *Loriculus galgulus* and geese; the collage was assembled by Muhammad Baqir in the 18th c.
  - The *Loriculus* is Jahangir's bird "from the country of Zirbad" (Tuzuk I, p. 272, 1614).
  - Den Hengst says the other birds are not by Mansur. The page's mixed dates therefore say nothing about the dodo's date.

## 8 Oct 2026: Persian text of the Cambay and Goa passages (page images read)

- **Editions.** Hashim ed. (Tehran 1980; archive.org `JahangirNamaTuzukEJahangiri-NooruddinMuhammadJahangirGurkaniFarsi`): Cambay p. 237, Goa pp. 122–123. 1863 lithograph, probably Sayyid Ahmad Khan's (archive.org `dli.ernet.450394`): Cambay p. 206, Goa p. 105. Images are in scratchpad/persian/.
- **Cambay, 10 Day, regnal year 12.** چند غراب از بنادر فرنگ … روز یکشنبه دهم غرابها را آراسته به نظر درآوردند و رخصت گرفته متوجه مقصد شدند.
  - Literal: "several ghurabs from the *banādir-i Farang*" (the Farang ports, i.e. the Portuguese-held ports) had traded at Cambay. On Sunday the 10th "having adorned the ghurabs [they] brought [them] into view, and having taken leave, set off".
  - **Only the boats were shown.** There is no peshkash, rarity or animal. The owners are not named.
  - The "port of Goa" in Thackston's rendering of this passage is a misreading; the Persian has Gogha (گوو / کوکه).
  - Thackston (p. 241) leaves out the Sunday sentence altogether.
  - **Downgrade:** the Cambay episode shows the emperor in direct contact with Portuguese-port shipping in Dec 1617, but records no transfer of any object.
- **Goa, 1612.** Muqarrab Khan was ordered to go to Goa, see the وزیری "who is governor of Goa" (Thackston: "the vice-rei"), and buy *nafāyis* (rarities) for the *sarkār-i khāṣṣa* (the emperor's private establishment).
  - The animal is called only *maymūn* (monkey): ash-grey wool, "from the ear-root to the chin red, wine-coloured", tail hanging like a cat's, a call like a fawn's.
  - Only two animals, the turkey and this one, are described as Goa purchases. The pheasant paragraph that follows is about Jahangir's own breeding experiment.

## 8 Oct 2026: English witnesses at court, 1616–18 (Roe, Terry, Coryate, factory letters)

There is **no dodo, ostrich or "strange fowle" given to Jahangir in 1616–18** in Roe (1899 and 1926 eds.), Terry (1625 and 1655/1777), Coryate, *Letters Received* IV–VI or *EFI* 1618–21. No Englishman saw the Cambay visit: Roe was at Ahmadabad from 15 Dec 1617, and his journal has a gap from 18 Dec to 8 Jan.

Verified near-misses (Roe 1926 ed., archive.org india.history.resource.111476; quotations checked against the downloaded text):
- **Live animals from Goa through Muqarrab Khan to the emperor, Mandu, Sept 1617.**
  - 9 Sept 1617 (Roe 1926 p. 380; 1899 II p. 414): the King "told me hee had some fat wild hogges sent him from Goa".
  - c. 23–25 Sept (p. 382; 1899 II p. 416): the King sent Roe "a fat hogge … sent up by Mochreb Chan, that came from Goa … since it came to the King it had eaten nothing but sugar and butter."
  - **This is the exact pipeline the hypothesis needs, documented in operation a year after the patache reached Goa:** Goa, then Muqarrab Khan, then live delivery to the court, then keeping and feeding at court. The animals named are hogs.
- **Portuguese embassy, Jan–Feb 1618.**
  - Roe to the Company, Ahmadabad, 14 Feb 1618 (Roe 1926 pp. 438, 454; 1899 II pp. 471, 483): "the King beeing neare the sea, the Viceroy (which never before was done) sent an ambassador". He waited at "Baltasare" (Bulsar) and got "as far as Cambaya".
  - He was refused "principally because his presents were not of great valew". The presents are not described.
  - Foster: the embassy "does not appear to be mentioned by the Portuguese historians". This is a Portuguese gift-bearing embassy aimed at Jahangir in Gujarat whose present list is lost. **Lead:** the outgoing letters of the new viceroy, D. João Coutinho, Conde de Redondo (arrived Goa late 1617), and *Assentos do Conselho do Estado* I (1618).
- **Muqarrab Khan gathering "toyes and rarityes to please the King"** before the King reached Gujarat, mid-Nov 1617 (John Browne, Ahmadabad, 10 Feb 1618; *EFI* 1618–21 p. 9). He also bought English goods worth about Rs 500.
- **Portuguese rarities to the court as a yearly custom.** Roe, Ajmer, 28 May 1616 (1926 p. 161): Portuguese came from Goa "with many rarietyes to sell the King and with rich presents from the Vizeroy of Goa … yearly theyr custome at this season." By 1617, however, the Goa cáfila had failed (18 June 1617) and Prince Khurram was seizing Portuguese goods.
- **The only bird presented to the King in 1617–18 that we found:** the Dutch, 13 Jan 1618, at Ahmadabad: "China ware, sanders, parrots and cloaves" (1926 p. 427). The Dutch account (Ravesteyn, Hague Transcripts) is not yet checked.
- **Painters with the camp.** Roe, Feb 1618: "The King being in the feild, all the workmen, both of the court and the great men, are retourned to Agra." This may bear on whether Mansur was with the camp in Gujarat.

## 8 Oct 2026: where the Portuguese manuscripts went

Agent report; key snippets in scratchpad/ameal/gbooks-snippets/.

- **Copyist.** Dr Teixeira de Carvalho of Coimbra, per Grandidier II p. 105 n. 1. The codex had 32 fascicles in a 17th-c. binding lettered "Découverte de l'île Saint-Laurent". Ameal bought it at the Conde de Linhares sale. Fascicle 6 (*Da segunda Missão*) lacked a leaf.
- **1924 Ameal sale.** Lot **2796** (catalogue pp. 744–745; Bechtloff 2002). It covered the Mariano roteiro, the "Relação da Jornada", and "Correspondência vária respeitante à Missão de S. Lourenço", codex pp. 69–219. The annotated copy at the Biblioteca Central da Marinha, Lisbon, has the pencil note **"342$00 Matos Sequeira"** (Leitão 1970). By 1970 the family no longer had it. It has not resurfaced, though other Ameal codices reached the BNP via the trade in 1990.
- **Leitão's Portuguese 1616 text is NOT from Ameal.** It is the "Relação do 2.º descobrimento que o S.or Vice-Rey Dom Jeronimo d'Azevedo mandou fazer à Ilha de São Lourenço o anno de 1616", anonymous. Leitão found it in 1965 in Marinha photocopies of the **Colección Salazar y Castro, Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid**; the shelfmark is in Leitão and not yet seen. Parish's "We met emas…" (p. 280) comes from this text.
  - It is probably a second witness of the *Segunda Missão* summary, which lacks Almeida's capture detail. **Its Portuguese for the fauna has "emas", not the capture sentence.**
  - Leitão translated other parts back into Portuguese from Grandidier.
- **Grandidier's archives.** MNHN Ms 2771–2823 and 3215–3270 have no Portuguese transcriptions or COACM working papers. The best remaining chance is the Fonds Grandidier at PBZT Tsimbazaza, Antananarivo (taken there in 1960, about 15,000 items, including "copies … de documents d'archives"; not catalogued online).
- **ARSI.** The DHCJ "Madagascar" entry cites **ARSI Goa 33 II, ff. 655, 725**. These are the likeliest originals of the 1616–17 mission letters and are not digitized.
- **Net.** "Nous avons pris…" survives only in Grandidier's French. Routes to the Portuguese, in order of likelihood:
  1. ARSI Goa 33 II, if Almeida's report or a copy is there;
  2. PBZT Tsimbazaza, for Teixeira de Carvalho's copies;
  3. the Matos Sequeira heirs or the trade, for the Ameal codex.

## 8 Oct 2026: Persian chronicle sweep

**Sources**
- *Iqbalnama-yi Jahangiri* III (Calcutta 1865; archive.org `IqbalNamaEJahangiriFarsi`), regnal years 11–22.
- Muhammad Hadi's continuation of the *Tuzuk* (appendix to the Hashim ed.), years 20–22.
- Method: OCR keyword search, with visual checks of the hits.

**Result**
- No dodo, ostrich, cassowary or unidentified large exotic bird.
- Nothing on animals from Goa or the Firangis. The only Firangi hit is "rubies liked by the Franks" in the Nauruz gifts of year 11.

**The 1624–27 chroniclers did record curiosities**
- A **huma** (lammergeier) shot alive in the Pir Panjal, examined and weighed; Iqbalnama pp. 246–247, read from the image; year 20, c. Oct 1625.
- A yellow **lion that lived with a goat**, a peshkash from Dawar Bakhsh.
- A white **falcon** from Shah Abbas's envoy.

**Consequence for the dating argument.** Stresemann and Das dated the painting to 1624–27 because the memoirs supposedly lapse in those years. They do not. Mu'tamad Khan and Hadi cover 1624–27 and note animal "wonders" (*gharāyib*), yet record no dodo. **So the silence argument favours 1624–27 no more than any other period:** the dodo's arrival is unrecorded in every Persian narrative of the reign.

**Limits**
- Years 11–19 rest on noisy OCR, so this is a weaker negative.
- Shah Jahan's chronicles (Lahori, Kanbo) were **not checked**: no usable Persian OCR is online. This needs local tesseract or HathiTrust full-text search behind its Cloudflare check.

## 8 Oct 2026: Shah Jahan chronicles (local tesseract OCR, Persian)

**Sources**
- Lahori, *Padshahnama* I: archive.org `padshahnama-volume-1`, 939 pp., regnal years 1–10, 1628–38.
- Kanbo, *ʿAmal-i Salih* I: archive.org `in.ernet.dli.2015.281684`, 654 pp.
- OCR text is in scratchpad/ocr/{lahori,kanbo}/txt/.

**Searches**: مرغ, جانور, طیور, پرنده, طاوس, شترمرغ, فیلمرغ, بوقلمون; Europeans/Surat (فرنگ, انگریز/انگلیس, ولندیز/هلند, سورت); rarity words (غرایب, نوادر, عجیب, طرفه).

**Result: no dodo or exotic bird.** The hits are:
- Lahori p. 447: a Nizami verse about Alexander, with a "bird that laid a golden egg" (poetic).
- Ring hunts (*qamargha*).
- White elephants.
- The Hugli campaign against the Firangis.
- Kanbo p. 58: a quadruplet birth at Surat reported as a "strange event".
- Kanbo: a Tibetan wild "goat" (shawl wool).
- Lahori pp. 397–400: people sent to Ahmadabad for "rarities" (*gharāyib*) for Dara Shikoh's wedding, 1633. The rarities are not itemised.

**Limit**: OCR keyword search, not a page-by-page read. But both chronicles do record animal wonders, so the silence carries some weight.

**Overall**: no Persian court narrative from 1616 to 1638 mentions a dodo.

**Attribution note (Stresemann 1958, pp. 448–449).**
- In 1958 the Berlin specialists W. Dudzus and E. Kühnel dated the original "im 17. Jahrhundert … vermutlich um die Mitte des Jahrhunderts oder sogar noch früher" (17th century, probably around mid-century or even earlier).
- Stresemann himself treated the leaf as a copyist's composition after lost originals.
- The Mansur attribution dates from Das 1973 and rests on style. The leaf is unsigned.

## 8 Oct 2026: the Surat dodos, carrier candidates (Foster, EFI 1624–29 and 1630–33)

Parish (2013, pp. 36–37) says only that the birds were "brought to India by English merchants". I found no published attempt to identify the ship.

**Ships that went Mauritius → Surat while Mundy was at Surat** (Sept 1628 – Nov 1630; May 1633 – early 1634):

1. **Hall's fleet** (*Mary, Hart, Star, Hopewell* and a frigate)
   - At Mauritius 28 May – 18 June 1628; anchored at Swally on 30 Sept 1628 (EFI 1624–29 pp. 261–269).
   - Altham's letter of 18 June 1628 proves this fleet took **live** dodos: he sent one to England by the *William*.
   - Mundy arrived at Swally on the *Expedition* that same week.
   - Context: in early 1628 the Surat factors had written "We have not seen any rare birds in these parts!" (EFI 1624–29 p. 210). This answered Company orders to procure rare birds and beasts.
2. ***Discovery* and *Reformation*** (John Vian's account, Marine Records vol. lii, p. 31)
   - At Mauritius 4–19 June 1630 (Turtle Bay, "plenty of provisions").
   - Then St Augustine's Bay and Johanna; they rejoined Morton/Rastell's fleet from England.
   - Anchored off Swally on 22–23 Sept 1630 (EFI 1630–33 pp. 44–46).
   - **On 24 Sept 1630 "Messrs. Skibbow, Barber, and Mundy came aboard the Fames"**, so Mundy personally met this incoming fleet before leaving for Agra in Nov 1630.
3. ***Fames*** (1632)
   - At Mauritius 3 Feb – 12 June 1632, then St Augustine's Bay.
   - Whether she returned to Surat is not checked. Mundy was away from Surat until May 1633.

**Ruled out**: the 1629 *Hart/Expedition/Hopewell* fleet (Mauritius June–July 1629) went on to England.

**Court presents.** The 1628–29 Agra/court agent "must be furnished with rarities and toys" for presents to the King and the Khankhanan (EFI 1624–29 p. ~255). No live bird is named among English presents in 1628–33. Animals shipped to England from Surat in 1629 were antelopes, and a horse and greyhounds went to Bantam.

**Decisive documents**:
- Marine Records journals: BL IOR/L/MAR/A vols. xlvi–xlvii for Hall 1628; lii and liii for 1630.
- Original Surat letters of Oct 1628 – 1631: IOR/E/3/12–13.
- Surat factory records: IOR/G/36.
- Foster calendars summaries only, so the bird lading may be in the originals. Check whether Adam Matthew's *East India Company* database (UCSC?) has the E/3 and L/MAR images.
