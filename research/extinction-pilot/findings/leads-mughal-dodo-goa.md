# Leads: the Mughal dodo, Goa, and Portuguese records of Mauritius birds (1600–1660)

Compiled 1 October 2026. Working texts were downloaded to the session scratchpad (`.../scratchpad/portuguese/`) and searched with grep. All page references come from the printed editions linked below. Quotations come from public-domain editions (Temple 1914/1919, Rogers and Beveridge 1909, Foster 1909, Grandidier 1904). The Grandidier passages are in his French translation of Portuguese manuscripts. I have not seen the Portuguese originals.

## 1. Summary

- **Chronology.** Jahangir died in October 1627. The two live dodos Peter Mundy saw were in the English Company's house at Surat ("Suratt howse"), and he did not reach Surat until late September 1628. Stresemann (1958) and Salim Ali (1968) already noted this. Some secondary accounts still say that Jahangir kept dodos at Surat (Wikipedia's "Dodo" article does). Mundy says neither. Two hypotheses therefore remain open:
  - **H1:** a bird reached Jahangir before October 1627. This fits the usual c.1625 dating and the Mansur attribution. The Portuguese route via Goa is the main unexamined path.
  - **H2:** the painting shows a Surat bird of 1628 or later. In that case it belongs to Shah Jahan's reign, and the Mansur attribution and c.1625 date would need revisiting.
- **Lead for H2 (probably partly known).** Captain John Hall's English fleet (*Mary*, *Hart*, *Star*, *Hopewell*) lay at Mauritius from 28 May to 18 June 1628. Emmanuel Altham wrote his dodo letter from there on 18 June 1628, and Herbert's dodo account comes from the same visit. The fleet anchored at Swally (Surat) on 30 September 1628. Mundy arrived at Swally on the *Expedition* in the same week. This is the most economical source for Mundy's two "Suratt howse" dodos. The Surat factors had written in early 1628 that they had found no rare birds to send home. The Company had standing orders to collect "strange birds and beasts" as presents.
- **Lead for H1 (Portuguese, possibly underused).** In March 1616 a Goa-based Jesuit expedition at Mauritius captured a young dodo, described as a young "ostrich" larger than a turkey. One of its two ships, the patache, returned to Goa on 1 September 1616. The visit is in Cheke and Parish's Table A1 (1616, "Almeida", *ema*). I found no discussion of the live capture or of whether the bird went on to Goa. This is the most concrete documented link between a live dodo and a Goa-bound Portuguese ship before 1627.
- **New Portuguese visit (no fauna).** Bocarro's *Década 13* records a second Goa-based visit to "Cirne" by Manuel Freire de Andrade's caravel, c.1617. The crew raided a Dutch salvage camp. No birds are mentioned, so Table A1 rightly omits it. It does show repeated Goa–Mauritius contact in 1616–17.
- **Negative results.** The *Livros das Monções* for 1605–1624 (Bulhão Pato and Silva Rego, full transcriptions on the BND) and the summaries for 1625–1631 (Matos) contain no reference to Cirne/Mauritius birds, dodos or ostriches. The Tuzuk-i Jahangiri, Roe's embassy journal, della Valle's India letters, Maclagan and the *Assentos do Conselho do Estado* vol. I (1618–33) are also silent on dodos.

## 3. Primary-source findings

### 3.1 Peter Mundy (Temple ed., Hakluyt Society)

- **Vol. II (1914), p. 318, log of 22 March 1634, homeward on the *Mary*.** After describing Mauritius's dodos he writes "I saw two of them in Suratt howse that were brought from thence." "Suratt howse" is the English Company's factory house. [archive.org/details/travelsofpetermu02mund](https://archive.org/details/travelsofpetermu02mund)
- **Vol. III pt 2 (1919), p. 352, Mauritius, 1638.** "Allthough wee now Mett with None… having seene 2 att Suratt broughtt From hence". The birds were as big as great turkeys, covered in down, with useless "hanguing wings". [archive.org/details/travelsofpetermu32mund](https://archive.org/details/travelsofpetermu32mund)
- **When he could have seen them.** Mundy arrived at Swally/Surat in late September 1628 on the *Expedition*. He was at Surat until his departure for Agra in November 1630, and again from May 1633 to early 1634 (Temple's introduction to vol. II).
- **Also in vol. II, p. 307.** At Agra he saw "amonge the Kings Fowle" one turkey (or guinea fowl) and a horned parrot (a cockatoo, per Temple). This shows that Shah Jahan's aviary, c.1631–32, held exotic birds that came by sea.

### 3.2 Hall's 1628 fleet: the likeliest source of the Surat dodos

The source is Foster, *English Factories in India 1624–1629* (1909), pp. 263–269, which calendars the ships' journals in IOR Marine Records vols. xlvi–xlvii. [archive.org/details/india.history.resource.88780](https://archive.org/details/india.history.resource.88780)

- **Outward.** The fleet left Swally on 12 March 1628 and raided the Konkan and Malabar coast, passing Goa on 6 April. It reached Mauritius on 27/28 May. The homeward *William* came in on 7 June. All sailed on 18 June, and the *William* parted for England on 26/27 June.
- **Return.** The fleet went via St Augustine's Bay, Mozambique and Johanna, and anchored at **Swally on 30 September 1628**. It found the *Jonas* and *Expedition* "newly arrived from England"; Mundy was on the *Expedition*.
- **Altham.** His letter (Morgan Library MA, ["Autograph letter… Mauritius… 1628 June 18"](https://www.themorgan.org/literary-historical/300983)) says he is sending a dodo home by the *William*. The letter and Herbert's account come from this same stay.
- **Context in the Surat records.**
  - Surat factors, early 1628 (EFI 1624–29, p. 210): "We have not seen any rare birds in these parts!" They send antelopes instead.
  - Foster's note in EFI 1618–21, pp. 199–200: the Company "always impressed upon their servants" the duty of bringing home "strange birds and beasts for presentation to the King or the great men at court".
  - Roe's draft articles of 1618 bound the English to "procure rarities for the Great Mogul" (Foster ed., *Embassy*, 1899, I, p. xxi).
- **Assessment.** Stresemann and Salim Ali already proposed the EIC Surat factory as where Mundy saw the birds. Naming Hall's fleet as the probable carrier, with an arrival date of 30 September 1628, may be new in print. Check Parish 2013 first. If the painting shows one of these birds, it dates from after 1628.
- **Next documents.** The ships' journals (BL IOR/L/MAR/A/xlvi, xlvii; Austin in the *Hart*, Sayers in the *Hopewell*, Pashley's account) and Surat letters of Oct 1628 – 1629 (IOR/E/3/12–13, G/36) may record live birds landed or sent up-country as presents. Foster calendars only summaries.
- **Later English traffic.** English ships from Surat used Mauritius regularly from 1628 onward: *Hart*, *Expedition* and *Hopewell* in 1629 (EFI 1624–29, pp. 331–333); *Charles*, *Jonas* and the *Fames* in 1630–32 (EFI 1630–33, [archive.org/details/india.history.resource.88781](https://archive.org/details/india.history.resource.88781)). Before 1628, English Surat ships rarely called there.

### 3.3 The 1616 Portuguese expedition: a live dodo on a Goa ship

The source is Grandidier et al., *Collection des ouvrages anciens concernant Madagascar* (COACM), t. II (1904), French translations of Jesuit papers in the Ameal manuscript (Coimbra). [archive.org/details/collectiondesouv02gran](https://archive.org/details/collectiondesouv02gran)

- **The voyage.** A caravel (*N. S. da Esperança*; captain-major Pero d'Almeida Cabral; English pilot "Thomas Hervi") and a patache (João Cardoso de Pina) left Goa on 7 February 1616. They carried Jesuits Manuel de Almeida, Luís Mariano, Custódio da Costa and António d'Azevedo.
- **At Mauritius.** They sighted Mauritius ("Cirne") on 21 March and anchored for four days at a bay they named São Bento (Grand Port). They met two Dutch deserters.
- **The capture.** Almeida's report of 23/25 May 1616 (pp. 105–118; this passage p. 117) states that they took a very young "autruche", already bigger than an ordinary turkey. Grandidier glosses it as a dodo ("un Dronte ou Didus ineptus"). The verb is "pris" (took or caught), so the bird was evidently taken alive.
- **Other accounts.** Mariano's letter of 18 June 1616 (pp. 140–142) covers the stop without the bird. Faria y Sousa, *Asia Portuguesa* III, pt 3, ch. 14, paraphrased in COACM II p. 274, lists "autruches" among the island's fauna.
- **Return to Goa.** On 31 May the patache left the caravel and went via Sahadia and Mozambique. It "arriva en rade de Goa" on **1 September 1616**, carrying a Malagasy prince for the viceroy (Cardoso de Pina's narrative, pp. 207–208). The Jesuits later returned to Goa as well (Faria, via COACM p. 279).
- **What the texts do not say.** No text I read says which ship held the bird or whether it survived.
- **Assessment.** The visit is known (Table A1, 1616). The live capture, and a possible Goa arrival within Jahangir's reign, appear unexploited. There are 11 years between 1616 and 1627. A captured juvenile reaching Goa in 1616–17 is chronologically compatible with H1 if the bird lived long in captivity; dodos are thought to have been long-lived in captivity, though that is unverified here.
- **Next steps.**
  - Find the Portuguese originals: the Ameal codex, Coimbra, sold from the Linhares library, and its present location. Look for "abestruz/ema" and any mention of the bird on the return voyage. Cheke and Parish's "ema" comes from the anonymous *Da segunda Missão…* text in the same codex.
  - Check ARSI *Goa* 33 and 34 (Jesuit letters, 1616–17), which are not online.
  - Check the viceroy's letters of late 1616 to Lisbon in the *Livros das Monções*, BND tomo III/IV (1614–19). I found no hit, but the OCR is imperfect.

### 3.4 The c.1617 Portuguese visit (Bocarro)

- **Source.** António Bocarro, *Década 13 da Historia da India* (1876 ed.), vol. II, cap. CLXVI, pp. 677–680. [archive.org/details/b2935237x_0002](https://archive.org/details/b2935237x_0002)
- **What happened.** Manuel Freire de Andrade's caravel, with Jesuits Paulo Jovio and "Carreiro", reached "a [ilha] do Cirne" while looking for Diogo Rodrigues (Rodrigues Island). The crew found Dutchmen salvaging three wrecked ships with clove and pepper cargoes, took 17 prisoners and burned the stores. They shot a goat "tamanho como um novilho".
- **Assessment.** No native fauna is named. It adds a Portuguese visit, with a date and named people, to the Mauritius chronology. It is not a dodo record and is probably known to historians of Dutch Mauritius (Moree 1998).
- **Earlier visit in the same text.** The chapter also recalls Pero de Almeida Cabral's 1616 call.

### 3.5 Mughal sources

- **Tuzuk-i Jahangiri** (Rogers and Beveridge 1909–14; [vol. I](https://archive.org/details/tuzukijahangirio00jahauoft), [vol. II](https://archive.org/details/b29352423_0002)): no bird that could be a dodo.
  - Vol. I pp. 215–216: Muqarrab Khan's purchases at Goa, c.1612. They include a turkey; a "monkey" with a fox-like face, hawk-like eyes and ash-coloured woolly hair; and pheasants bred in captivity. The "monkey" is often identified as a Madagascar lemur; that identification is mine and the literature's, not the source's. This shows that by 1612 Goa supplied the court with western Indian Ocean island fauna.
  - Vol. II p. 201: the 1621 zebra, which the *Iqbalnama* says came by sea.
  - Jahangir's own memoir stops in his 17th regnal year (1622). Mu'tamad Khan's continuation runs to 1624. Nothing covers 1624–27, the period in which Salim Ali places the painting.
- **Other Persian chronicles to check:**
  - *Iqbalnama-i Jahangiri*, for 1624–27.
  - Lahori's *Padshahnama* and Kanbo's *ʿAmal-i Salih*, for 1628–31, to look for English or Dutch *peshkash* of birds from Surat.
  - None was checked here. Persian texts are digitized (e.g. Bibliotheca Indica editions on archive.org) but would need Persian OCR or LLM reading.

### 3.6 Dutch context (local GLOBALISE index, quick checks)

- **Inv. 1082, scan 0383.** GG Carpentier and Council, Batavia, to the Amsterdam Chamber, 28 Jan 1625. They promise to search "overal" for "Vremde vogels, Macke gedierten… rariteijten", as ordered. The VOC was gathering live rarities for Europe in 1624–25, just as dodos appear alive in the Netherlands (the van de Venne dodo, 1626). Not Mughal.
- **Inv. 1132, scan 1030.** Barent Pietersz., Surat, 19 Dec 1639, lists "1: a 2: casuwaris voogels" among presents to be sent for the court. Cassowaries were a routine Surat gift item, which supports reading the 1625 "casuwaris" for Jahangir as a real cassowary.
- **Inv. 1095, scan 0922.** Le Febvre's fleet, Jan 1629, got "velthoenders, dodeersen" at Mauritius. This is already in Table A1.
- **Not yet searched.** Van den Broecke's own Surat letters (inv. 1082, fol. 1–12, 1624, and adjacent volumes 1620–29). He drew dodos at Mauritius in 1617 and was VOC director at Surat in 1620–29. Read them for presents to Jahangir and Asaf Khan. This is the best Dutch path for H1.

## 5. Ranked next steps

1. **Check novelty before writing anything.** Read Parish 2013 (ch. on captive dodos, p. 74 and around) and Cheke and Hume 2008 on (a) Hall's 1628 fleet and Altham as the source of the Surat birds, and (b) the 1616 capture. Also read Stresemann 1958 for what he proposed about the route.
2. **VOC Surat letters, 1620–1629.** Search the local GLOBALISE index for van den Broecke's letters from Surat: catalogue `settlement='Surat'`, years 1620–29. Use full text on `schenkagie|present|vereering` combined with `vogel|dier|rariteyt`, and read the gift lists for Jahangir and Asaf Khan. The FTS tooling already exists. If a Mauritius bird reached the court before 1627, a Dutch Surat gift list is the likeliest place to find it.
3. **Hall's fleet journals and Surat letters, Sept 1628 – 1629.** These are BL IOR/L/MAR/A vols. xlvi–xlvii and IOR/E/3/12–13. Foster gives summaries only. If they were digitized (the Adam Matthew "East India Company" module covers E/3; check institutional access), grep for "Dodo", "fowle" and "Mauritius". This would date the Surat birds and might show whether any went up-country as *peshkash* to Shah Jahan.
4. **Portuguese originals of the 1616 voyage.**
   - Find the present location of the Ameal codex. The 1920s Ameal library sale catalogue may say; possible holders are the Biblioteca Nacional or the Universidade de Coimbra.
   - Find any edition of *Da segunda Missão…*. Portuguese texts of these Madagascar letters may also be in Wicki or Silva Rego, *Documentação… Padroado* (India series), or in Sommervogel-listed editions.
   - Look for words for the bird (abestruz/ema) and its fate on the return voyages of 1616–17.
5. **Jahangir's court, 1624–27.** Read the *Iqbalnama-i Jahangiri* (Bibliotheca Indica, Persian) for 1624–27, and Lahori's *Padshahnama* for 1628–31. Look for birds offered by "Firangis" from Goa or Surat. This needs Persian OCR or an LLM reader. Search terms: *shuturmurgh* (ostrich), *murgh-i ʿajib*, *pishkash-i Firangi*.
6. **The painting itself.** Get the current catalogue entry for the St Petersburg Muraqqa folio at the Institute of Oriental Manuscripts (IOM RAS). The [UNESCO Memory of the World nomination](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/russia_miniatures_eng.pdf) may give the folio number and dating. Establish whether the c.1625 date and the Mansur attribution rest on style alone. If so, H2 (a Surat bird after 1628) cannot be excluded.
7. **Lower priority: Portuguese *relações* (BND).** Search printed *relações* of 1610–1650 carrack voyages that touched "Cirne" or "Mascarenhas" (for example the *História Trágico-Marítima* narratives), using full-text keyword search (Cirne, ema/abestruz). Expect low yield. The Livros das Monções result suggests Portuguese official correspondence did not note the island's birds.

## Update, 7 October 2026

- **VOC Surat letters, 1614–1630 (GLOBALISE, local FTS).** Searches for dodo names, Mauritius with birds, cassowaries or ostriches with the Mughal court. No dodo at Surat or Agra. Only known hits (1615 Wapen van Amsterdam; Le Febvre 1629) and the 1625 cassowary for Jahangir (inv. 1084 scan 0134, 1090 scan 0626, 1093 scans 0108/0128). Incidental: inv. 1113 scan 0642 (volume 1628–35) lists "1 vogel struijs gestorven" in a ship's consumption list with Portuguese crew names; context not read.
- **Lettere annue del Giappone, China, Goa, et Ethiopia (Naples: Scoriggio, 1621), Besson Collection, University of Tsukuba** (IIIF manifest https://dc.tulips.tsukuba.ac.jp/iiif/3/rb_10079324184/manifest; scans 48–68 read). Despite the title (1615–1619), the Goa letters are for **1618** (pp. 94–109, signed Gaspar Luís, Goa, 1 Feb 1619) and **1619** (from p. 110), plus "Missione del Mogor" (pp. 131–135). No birds, rarities, Mauritius or gifts to the emperor. Fr Manuel Pinheiro set out again for the Mughal court "con mandati del Vicerè" but was forced back to Goa (pp. 111–112). The 1619 letter also reports a failed attempt at a residence on "San Lorenzo" (Madagascar) by two Fathers from Mozambique (pp. 130–131).
- **Consequence.** The Goa annual letters for 1616–17, which would cover the return of the 1616 Mauritius/Madagascar expedition, are not in this printed collection and appear to survive only in ARSI (*Goa* 33–34), not online.
- **Painting date.** The holding institution's date (c. 1615, Google Arts & Culture entry) is stated without rationale; the folio is a collage of birds from different sources, re-backed in Isfahan.
- **Monções do Reino.** The 62 Lisbon volumes (1605–1651) were given to the National Archives of India as images in 2017; no public online access found. The printed Bulhão Pato/Silva Rego transcriptions remain the searchable route and were negative (1 Oct).
