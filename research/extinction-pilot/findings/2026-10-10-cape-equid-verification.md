# Cape striped-equid leads: source checks, 10 October 2026

Internal research record. No public passage or species data was changed. Scan checks below were performed by Codex; they do not imply historian approval or a settled species identification.

## Wagenaer and council: 19 April 1665, not 1666

The date assigned provisionally in the 4 October findings is incorrect. The striped-equid passage in VOC 4000, scan 0639, continues directly onto scan 0640, where the letter closes at the Fort of Good Hope on **19 April 1665**. The date and the copied names of Z. Wagenaer, Abraham Gabbema, and Hendrick Lacus were visually checked against the manuscript scan. The inventory's 1664–1666 date range is not the date of this letter.

- [Passage, scan 0639](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/4000/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_4000_0639), manuscript p. 294.
- [Continuation and dated closing, scan 0640](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/4000/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_4000_0640).

The manuscript confirms the brown-stripe wording and the comparison of blue, yellow, reddish rays with a rainbow. However, the report begins with what people say about catching animals at waterholes and introduces the appearance with conditional wording, “want soo die paerden …”. It should not be rewritten as Wagenaer's direct observation of two securely identified species. There is no explicit description of an unstriped hindquarter in the passage. A brown-stripe/rainbow distinction alone does not establish quagga versus mountain zebra.

The letter reports that recent inland travellers shot such an animal and removed its skin with head and feet attached. The continuation says they discarded it because carrying it over mountains and valleys in the heat was too difficult. This is a reported kill and an unsuccessful attempt to bring a skin to the fort, not a completed shipment to Europe. The proposed live capture and European valuation remain prospective.

Only the relevant wording, narrative sequence and closing date were checked visually. The full GLOBALISE machine transcription remains uncorrected; it includes text from the underlying exposed manuscript leaf, visible in these photographs. Do not quote the whole machine-text page as a continuous letter.

Outstanding: compare the dated letter with Böeseken's *Dagregister en Briewe van Zacharias Wagenaer, 1662–1666* and Leibbrandt's published précis; identify the referenced inland journey; collate the full Dutch passage before making a publication transcription or translation. The earlier attribution to a 16 May 1666 letter is withdrawn. No novelty claim is established.

## Schrijver's expedition: 28 January 1689

The previously tentative expedition identification can now be made specific. The description occurs in Isaq Schrijver's journal, entry for **28 January 1689**, published by E. C. Godée Molsbergen in *Reizen in Zuid-Afrika in de Hollandse tijd*, III: *Tochten langs de Z.O.-kust en naar het Oosten 1670–1752* ('s-Gravenhage: Martinus Nijhoff, 1922), pp. 103–104. The journal begins on 4 January and ends on 10 April 1689.

- [Published Dutch text](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/gode006reiz03_01/gode006reiz03_01_0008.php).
- [Printed scan, pp. 98–123 with inserted plates](https://rhinoresourcecenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2006/12/1309485675.pdf): the passage crosses PDF pages 8–9, printed pp. 103–104. Both printed pages were rendered and visually inspected.
- [VOC 4027, scan 0226](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/4027/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_4027_0226): the photographed spread shows the Friday 28 January entry on the right-hand leaf, numbered 201. Its report of seeing and shooting a striped wild equid was visually compared with the print. “283” in the machine transcription is not the passage's manuscript page number.
- [Parallel copy, VOC 4029, scan 0375](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/invnr/4029/file/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_4029_0375): machine text only in this check; manuscript image not yet inspected. Do not count it as an independent observation.

Short extract from the printed edition, joining the line-end word break and the page break, with no internal omission:

> in 't passeren van dese kloov hebben wij een rare soort van wilde esels gesien, van dewelke wij er een schooten, hebbende de groote van een paard en bruijn en grau gestreept bevonden

The journal reports a horse-sized, brown-and-grey striped animal seen and shot while crossing the mountain pass it calls Lange Kloov. The entry continues with the naming of a valley Goede Hoop and gives the predominant course as **O.N.O. (east-northeast)**. The earlier findings' north-northeast summary is corrected. Modern coordinates have not been established from the route.

The passage does not name a quagga or describe the distribution of stripes over the body. Retain it as a striped-equid observation with unresolved species/subspecies identification. The published edition already contains the passage: it is a recovered known source, not a newly discovered account. Whether particular quagga histories cite or identify it remains to be checked. The editor's introductory summary calls the animals zebras; this is later editorial interpretation, not the journal's taxonomic wording.

Outstanding: read Mossop's 1931 edition and its route/species annotations; inspect and collate the VOC 4029 copy; compare specialist quagga and Cape mountain zebra literature. The 1922 edition's archival locator is historical and is not itself a demonstrated concordance to today's VOC inventory numbers.
