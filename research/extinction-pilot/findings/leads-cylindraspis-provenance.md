## Unverified points of departure (not from the papers read)

From memory of the taxonomic literature; check each in Bour et al. 2014 before use.
- *Testudo vosmaeri* Fitzinger is named after Arnout Vosmaer, who described a live Rodrigues tortoise in the Stadholder's collection in the 1770s. Whether RMNH 6001 is that animal, and whether it passed through Paris after 1795, is unverified.
- *Testudo indica* Schneider is often tied to the tortoise dissected by Claude Perrault's Académie group (published 1670s), said to be from "Coromandel". Whether MNHN 7819 is that specimen is unverified.
- *Testudo schweigeri* Gray, 1830/31: Gray's description and the BM register for 1876.10.28 should name the source of the specimen. Not checked.

## Gaps and documentary records that could close them

1. **NHMUK 1876.10.28.4 (schweigeri type): island, collector, route.** BM(NH) zoology register for 28 Oct 1876 (NHM Archives; registers are digitised for some departments). Gray 1830/31 text and plate (BHL). If the specimen was in London by 1830, a Mauritius origin before c. 1735–1760 implies a long cabinet history: look for sale catalogues of 18th-century cabinets in Delpher and BHL. *Tractability: high for the register and Gray (one archive request, one BHL lookup).*
2. **MNHN hatchling (MNHN-RA-0.1991) and the second hatchling: collector and date.** MNHN reptile catalogue (Bour has published on its history); Paris catalogue numbers in the "0." series are retrospective. French Rodrigues visitors with collecting habits: the 1761 Venus transit party (Pingré) and Île de France naturalists (Commerson, Sonnerat). ANOM and Archives nationales Marine series, MNHN central archives (Fonds Commerson). *Tractability: medium; depends on Bour's chapter, which probably already states what is known.*
3. **NMW 1461: basis for "late 18th century".** Vienna Herpetology inventory; Vienna imperial collection purchases (e.g. from the 1806 Leverian sale or Dutch cabinets). *Tractability: medium; an email to NMW Herpetology would likely settle it.*
4. **RMNH 6001 and the Vosmaer tortoise.** Vosmaer's 1770s descriptions (BHL, Delpher), the Stadholder's cabinet inventories, and the 1795 French seizure lists. *Tractability: high; printed and digitised.*
5. **MNHN 7819, 7831, 9374, 1883.558: entry routes.** MNHN registers; for 1883.558, the 1883 donor. *Tractability: medium.*
6. **Dates of extinction (Mauritius c. 1735).** For Mauritius the VOC record to 1710 gives a terminus for decline: GLOBALISE, already indexed. For 1721–1767: Compagnie des Indes correspondence (ANOM C/4; Archives nationales Marine), which Cheke and Bour probably used. Rodrigues: Leguat (1708), the 1761 transit party, and the Île de France tortoise-transport records (ANOM). Réunion c. 1840: colonial administrative papers and *Feuille hebdomadaire de l'île Bourbon*. *Tractability: Mauritius VOC high (in hand); French series low to medium (unindexed manuscript).*
7. **VOC-period live exports.** Any tortoise sent alive from Mauritius to Batavia, the Cape or the Netherlands before 1710 is a candidate origin for a cabinet specimen such as the schweigeri juvenile. Lexical search of GLOBALISE for "schildpad" with "levendig/lewendig", "gesonden", "Caap", in Mauritius volumes. *Tractability: high; one query pass.*

## Bearing of our VOC finds (2026-09-30 run)

- **Urbanus to Lamotius, 26 Aug 1683 (4019_0155–0157).** About 20 live land tortoises taken on the EIC President for St Helena. Live exports from Mauritius by English ships in the 1680s are a possible route by which a juvenile Mauritius tortoise reached a British collection. It does not link to NHMUK 1876.10.28.4 directly (the President was wrecked; the Persia Merchant reached England), but it shows the route existed. Worth checking whether the Persia Merchant also carried tortoises.
- **Diodati 1698 (4039_0031–0032), Hugo 1673–74 (4011_0403, 0409–0410, 0415; 4010_0893–0895), Van Laar 1667–68 (4003_1614–1623), Wreede 1670 (4006_1054), 1672–73 (4008_0845; 4010_0931–0933).** Dated harvest counts and islet populations. They bear on the Mauritius extinction chronology: "almost gone" on the mainland by 1698, with islet populations (thousands in 1670; 320 brought back alive in 1674) as the likely survivors. That supports treating "c. 1735" as the end of islet and remnant populations rather than of mainland abundance; it does not date the last animal.
- **Hugo, May 1674 (4011_0415):** 320 tortoises brought back *alive* from an islet. Live animals in Dutch hands are a precondition for any live export. Which species lived on the islets is unknown.
- None of our finds names a specimen, a collector shipping tortoise shells to Europe, or a museum.
