# Leads: the 1647 dodo in Japan

Researched 1 October 2026. Transcriptions from GLOBALISE are machine HTR (OCR) and have not been checked against the scans unless I say so. Japanese passages are my readings of NDL OCR text, and one was checked against the page image (marked below). A Japanese-reading collaborator should verify every Japanese reading before anything here goes on the site.

## 1. What is already established

**Winters, R. & Hume, J. P. (2015).** "The dodo, the deer and a 1647 voyage to Japan." *Historical Biology* 27(2): 258–264. https://doi.org/10.1080/08912963.2014.884566. The article is closed access (OpenAlex: `oa_status: closed`). Tandfonline, ResearchGate and Academia all returned HTTP 403, so I could not read the full text. The UvA record (https://dare.uva.nl/search?identifier=13ed15f7-2982-497f-9777-85f89e9d496a) has only the abstract.

- **Claim, from the abstract:** a live dodo was sent to Japan in 1647. The authors call it the last known captive bird.
- **Sources:**
  - The Deshima day register of 2 Sept 1647, written under Willem Verstegen.
  - The warehouse/negotieboek entry. A figure caption reads "The dodo, deer and bezoar (highlighted…) (1 witte hinde, 1…)".
  - The published English summary in Viallé & Blussé, *The Deshima Dagregisters* XI (Intercontinenta 23, 2001), which is in their reference list.
  - Other references (Crossref) include Millies 1868 and Heeringa 1895 (*De Indische Gids*). These relate to the Batavia letter announcing the shipment.
- **Fate of the bird:** every secondary summary agrees that Winters & Hume leave it unknown.
- **Citing works worth checking:**
  - Winters, "The Dutch East India Company and the Transport of Live Exotic Animals…" (2020). https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-42595-1_2 (closed).
  - Hume, "The changing face of the dodo" (2021). https://doi.org/10.1080/08912963.2021.1940996

**Kawabata Hiroto (川端裕人), *ドードーをめぐる堂々めぐり：正保四年に消えた絶滅鳥を追って* (Iwanami, 2021).** https://www.iwanami.co.jp/book/b593200.html. This is the main follow-up from the Japanese side, and it must be read before we claim novelty for anything below. Chapter 1 has these sections:

- "ドードーは徳川家光に会ったのか？" (Did the dodo meet Iemitsu?)
- "松山、福岡、佐賀と数百年にわたる縁" (Matsuyama, Fukuoka, Saga: centuries of connection)
- "長崎、出島動物園にて" (Nagasaki, at the Dejima animal collection)

His notes are online as a free PDF: https://www.iwanami.co.jp/files/moreinfo/0614970/bibliography_0614970.pdf. They show what he checked:

- The Batavia letter, the day register, the factuur and the negotie journaal, all seen in the Shiryō Hensanjo copies at the University of Tokyo with help from Isabel Tanaka-van Daalen.
- Matsuyama sources (松山叢談, 松山市史, 松山市史料集 3). For the last of these he says he found nothing about animals.
- Saga (Chōkokan) sources.
- Inoue Masashige's domain records (Takaoka).
- Nagasaki chronicles: 寛宝日記と犯科帳 and 長崎実録大成.
- Isono's chronologies. He notes that Isono's 日本博物誌総合年表 (2012) "missed" the 1647 dodo and white deer.
- Dejima excavation reports, looking for faunal remains.

His notes do not show whether he used the *Tokugawa jikki* entry in §3A below. The "Did it meet Iemitsu?" section suggests he looked at shogunal records.

Kawabata also quotes the Batavia letter of July 1647, which sends a white deer and a Mauritius dodo "as a token of affection" (親愛のしるしとして). It went from the governor-general to the Japan factory. The letter has been known since Millies 1868 and was cited by Hachisuka (1953). Hachisuka's enquiry to Nagasaki Library about it was made in 1941.

## 2. Dutch sources after 2 September 1647

### 2A. The rest of the 1646–47 day register (GLOBALISE, NA 1.04.02 inv. 1165, OBP copy)

These entries do not appear in the abstract-level summaries of Winters & Hume. Kawabata's notes suggest he knew the parrot entry, because Murakami translated it (see 2C).

- **Scan 0143, 1 Sept 1647.** Coyet is allowed to bring ashore "zijne kist met vlunderagie nevens het levende gedierke".
  - 2 Sept entry: as already known.
- **Scan 0145, 6 Sept, and 0146, 7–8 Sept.**
  - **6 Sept.** On the governor's orders, all rarities are locked in the warehouses so that "Zickingodo" cannot see them. This is Inoue Chikugo-no-kami Masashige (井上筑後守政重). The register remarks on great jealousy among the officials.
  - **6 Sept, the deer.** "Den heere van Faccatta het witte hert door den Gouverneur toegestaen zijnde…" The governor granted the white deer to the lord of Hakata. The Dutch felt they could not decently accept it as a gift, so they set a price "een ijtselio" (1 ryō = 4 mas 3 condrijn) with the burgemeester and interpreters.
  - **7 Sept.** The parrot is first requested for "Zikkingodo". The governor orders the interpreters to declare that "genigh gedierte mocht werden vercoft" (no animals may be sold) unless the governor did not order them kept "voorde Maij:t" (for the shogun). The parrot comes back to the Dutch for the moment.
  - **8 Sept.** "Naerdemiddagh door ordre des Gouverneurs, wert gemelte pavegaij nevens nogh eenich becomen gevogelt Matsenatijao Okiosamma vanden bloede des keijsers, heere over de vier provintien van 't Eijlandt Cikoko overhandicht." On the governor's order, the parrot and "some other birds received" were handed to Matsudaira Oki-sama, "of the shogun's blood, lord over the four provinces of Shikoku".
    - Identification: Matsudaira Oki-no-kami Sadayuki (松平隠岐守定行), lord of Iyo-Matsuyama and the shogunal commander for the 1647 Portuguese crisis.
    - **This is the only post-2 Sept passage where an unnamed bird could be the dodo.** "Becomen gevogelt" (birds received or obtained) is ambiguous. The register does not say the dodo was among them, so that remains a hypothesis.
- **Scans 0147–0159** run to the end of the register on 10 Oct 1647. I searched them for dod-, vogel, gedier-, hert, rarit-, Faccatta, Jedo and schenck-. The only further hits are the rarities shown to Inoue (16–18 Sept, scans 0147–0150) and the remark that globes and maps were judged unsuitable as gifts. There are no further animal entries.

### 2B. Inventory 1164 (Taiwan/Japan volume, OBP)

- **Scan 0738.** "Extract uijt de lopende negotieboecken des comptoirs Nangasackij", 29 Aug 1647, catalogue fol. 658–663. Under "huijs oncosten … ongetaxeert" it lists "1 dodeers", "1. pedro porco" (bezoar) and "1 witte hinde" among the goods received from Batavia via Taiwan on *Den Jongen Prins*. This is evidently the source of the Winters & Hume figure.
  - Note that the dodo was booked as an unvalued household item, not as merchandise, and that the deer was a hind.
- **Scans 0710–0717.** Verstegen's letter of 12 Oct 1647, catalogue fol. 648–653, describes the 1647 mobilisation.
  - The Shikoku lord appears here as "matsendaijro okio".
  - "Saeken desen Jaere verschenen zijn meest alle tot schenckagie … nodigh geweest": most of the goods that arrived this year were needed for gifts.
  - There is no mention of the dodo.
- **Resolutions, 21 Aug–9 Oct 1647** (fol. 655–657), and the **return factuur of *Den Jongen Prins*, 12 Oct 1647** (fol. 664). No dodo, deer or bird terms appear in the HTR. The factuur result suggests the bird did not leave Japan on that ship, but HTR misses are possible.

**NFJ 1166, what I read.** The volume has 11 scans. I downloaded them via IIIF and read scans 0003–0007 (left half) and 0009–0010 (left half) myself. These cover expenses from Nagasaki to Osaka and Edo and the start of the return journey, plus the gifts to the Osaka governors and the 3 Dec 1648 gifts at Nagasaki (textiles).

- Food, porters, packing mats, cases and a large mirror carried from Osaka to Edo are all itemised.
- I found **no expense for carrying, caging or feeding a live bird or animal**.
- This is weak evidence that Coyet did not take the dodo to Edo in winter 1647–48. It is not proof: gifts could be costed elsewhere.
- I did not read scans 0007 (right half) and 0008 (return journey).

## 3. Japanese sources: search results

Tool: the NDL "Next Digital Library" lab API (`https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/search?keyword=…`). Repeating the `keyword` parameter gives book-level AND. Per-book OCR text comes from `…/api/book/fulltext/{pid}` (a zip). The tool covers public-domain printed books to about 1968 and about 74k OCR'd pre-modern books. The pre-modern OCR is poor.

### 3A. Most significant find: Kuroda's white deer and a white bird reached the shogun

**徳川実紀 (Tokugawa jikki), 大猷院殿御実紀 巻六十七, 正保四年九月** (Keizai Zasshisha ed., 1907, vol. 2). NDL pid 772966, frame 454: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/772966/1/454.

- **I checked the reading against the page image** (IIIF crop).
- 〇廿二日 … 松平筑前守忠之白鹿并白音呼鳥を献ず。 On the 22nd day, Matsudaira Chikuzen-no-kami Tadayuki presented a white deer and a white *inko* bird.
  - Matsudaira Chikuzen-no-kami Tadayuki is Kuroda Tadayuki 黒田忠之, the Dutch "heer van Faccatta". The Kuroda used the Matsudaira surname.
  - The entry is attributed to (日記). Tracing it is listed under "Bakufu diary" below.
- 〇廿三日 … またさきに松平筑前守忠之が献ぜし白鹿を。水戸黄門頼房卿へつかはさる. The white deer that Tadayuki had presented was then sent on to Tokugawa Yorifusa of Mito. Cited to (日記。水戸記。).
- **Date.** Shōhō 4/IX/22 is roughly 20–21 Oct 1647. I worked this from Murakami's own concordance, 正保四年十一月十八日 = 14 Dec 1647. Confirm with a calendar table.
  - Six weeks from the 6 Sept grant at Nagasaki fits the journey to Edo.
- **What it shows.** It traces the deer from Dejima to Iemitsu and then to the Mito house.
- **The bird.** It also records a *white bird* travelling with the deer. 白音呼鳥 means "white *inko*" (音呼 is a standard Edo spelling of インコ, parakeet or cockatoo). The likeliest reading is a white cockatoo.
  - The Dutch had given the shogun two white parrots (白鸚鵡) the year before, per secondary summaries.
  - Nothing in the Dutch record says Kuroda received the dodo. The register says the parrot and other birds went to Matsudaira Sadayuki.
  - **Interpretation, kept separate from what the source reports:** a dodo relabelled as an "inko" is possible but unlikely. Still, this is the only documented point where a bird plausibly from the 1647 Dutch shipment reaches Edo, so it deserves a check against the underlying 日記.
  - The bakufu diaries for the dodo and other birds: no hits for 鸚鵡/音呼/異鳥 near 正保四年 VII–IX in the jikki OCR, apart from this entry.
- **A tangent on the deer's afterlife** (interpretation): the Mito house kept exotic animals. 桃源遺事 (NDL pid 1030760, frame 75) has Tokugawa Mitsukuni releasing 白鹿 and other animals on his domain lands.

## 4. Japanese depictions

- **No Japanese depiction of a dodo is known.** Neither Kawabata nor Isono reports one.
- The only route by which a dodo image reached Japan is Jonston's *Historiae naturalis* (Dutch edition), which has the Clusius-derived dodo plate.
  - It was presented to the shogun in 1663, and Japanese artists copied its plates widely.
  - Noro Genjō's 阿蘭陀禽獣虫魚図和解 (NDL pid 2555966) covers only eagle, peacock, bird of paradise, pheasant and cassowary ("カズワール") among the birds, with no dodo (per Kawabata).
  - Any Japanese copy of the Jonston dodo plate would be a later copy of a European image, not an observation of the 1647 bird.
- **Image search with IIIF is technically feasible.** NDL serves IIIF for its holdings. Keio's 唐蘭船持渡鳥獣之図 and 外国産鳥之図 (excerpted in Isono & Uchida, 舶来鳥獣図誌, 1992) and TNM's 禽譜 (Hotta Masaatsu) have online images.
  - Yield is likely low. These albums are mostly 18th–19th century, a century after 1647.
  - A visual sweep of copies of Jonston plates for an unlabelled dodo is a modest, well-defined task.

## 5. Ranked next steps

1. **Read Coyet's original register NFJ 61 (Nov 1647–Dec 1648) and NFJ 60 (Sept 1647 entries) in the IIIF scans.** These are the only unread first-hand Dutch records covering the months after the bird landed. *Dutch palaeography; no Japanese needed.* Also finish NFJ 1166 (scans 0007 right half and 0008) and check the 1647–48 ledger NFJ 992 for how the "dodeers" account was closed.
2. **Download *The Deshima Diaries 1641–1660* (OA) by hand** and check the 8 Sept 1647 entry, the editors' notes, and Coyet's diary index for dodo, bird and Matsudaira. *English.*
3. **Trace the jikki entry of IX/22 to the bakufu diary (江戸幕府日記, 正保四年九月廿二日) and to 水戸記.** Do they describe the 白音呼鳥 further, give its origin (Dutch, via Nagasaki) or list other birds? *Needs a Japanese reader.*
4. **Kuroda records for Shōhō 4:** 黒田家譜 (Tadayuki's reign) in the Fukuoka Prefectural Library images, and the Fukuoka City Museum's Kuroda documents. Look for animals obtained at Nagasaki and the gift to the shogun. *Needs a Japanese reader, including kuzushiji for manuscripts.*
5. **Matsudaira Sadayuki records** (松山叢談, 久松家譜, Iyo-Matsuyama han documents at 愛媛県歴史文化博物館 / 伊予史談会) for the birds received at Nagasaki on 8 Sept 1647 (Shōhō 4/VII/9, approximate). This is the most direct Japanese test of the "dodo among the birds" hypothesis. Kawabata searched some of these and reports nothing; ask what he did not see. *Needs a Japanese reader.*
6. **Read Kawabata (2021), chapter 1, before writing anything for the site,** to avoid repeating his findings. Consider contacting him: he worked with Hume.
7. **Batavia letter of July 1647** (cited by Millies 1868, Hachisuka 1953, Winters & Hume). Get its archival reference and wording to see whether the dodo was meant for a named recipient. The Kawabata summary implies it was for the shogunate; this is unverified. *Dutch.*
8. **Low priority:** an IIIF visual sweep of Japanese copies of Jonston plates and of the Keio and TNM bird albums. This can only ever yield a derivative image.

## Update, 7 October 2026

- **Deshima Diaries 1641–1660 (Brill 2023, open access) read.** Verstegen chapter (doi:10.1163/9789004510210_011) and Coyett chapter (doi:10.1163/9789004510210_012), PDFs downloaded through Benjamin's Chrome to ~/Downloads.
  - 2 Sept 1647: "the deer and the dodo were taken to the council house and then returned"; the Lord of Hakata viewed them. 6 Sept: the white deer sold to him for 4 mas 3 candareens. 7 Sept: the governor ruled that animals could go to Chikugo-no-kami unless kept for the shogun. 8 Sept: "the parrot and some other birds were handed to Matsudaira Oki-no-kami".
  - Verstegen (same register) says animals "are subject to a sudden death here" and must be kept until the winter court journey; the Japanese "are tired of large animals".
  - The January 1647 audience gifts included a cassowary and two white cockatoos. This makes a white cockatoo the likely 白音呼鳥 that Kuroda sent to Edo with the white deer (Tokugawa jikki, Shōhō 4/IX/22).
  - Coyett's register, 3 Nov 1647–8 Dec 1648 (edition is an English rendering with goods lists abridged): no dodo, bird, cassowary, parrot or white deer. His court journey of Nov 1647–Feb 1648 carries no live animals.
- **Factory account books checked (NFJ 847 journal 1646–47, book S; NFJ 848 journal 1647–48, book T; NFJ 992 ledger downloaded, not read).** Scans via METS/IIIF; legible. NFJ 847 closing (ultimo Oct 1647, scan 020) books "Schenckagie" of 3917.17.5, including rarities worth 1412.14.5 taken by Commissioner Chikugo-no-kami, the governors and other lords. Values only; no items named. Because the dodo was booked "ongetaxeert", the accounts are unlikely to record its fate. NFJ 848 opens 6 Nov 1647 with carried-forward accounts "Meubelen van 't huys" and "Rariteyten" (532.16.x).
- **Assessment.** The published and accounting Dutch record is now effectively exhausted. The bird's fate, if recorded anywhere, is most likely in Japanese records of Matsudaira Sadayuki (Iyo-Matsuyama) or Inoue Masashige for Shōhō 4/VII–IX, both partly searched by Kawabata without result.
