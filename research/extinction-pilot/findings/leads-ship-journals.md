# Ship journals and voyage texts outside GLOBALISE: dodo leads, 1598–1700

Compiled 1 Oct 2026. This is a survey of accessible sources and a target list, not a set of verified findings. Transcriptions marked "read from image" were checked by eye against a page image; nothing else has been collated against an edition. Scratch files (search dumps, images, METS) are in the session scratchpad under `journals/` and are not committed.

Comparison baseline: Cheke and Parish 2020, *Quaternary* 3(4), Table A1 (PDF read, references checked). Its Mauritius sequence is 1598 (several), 1601 (Laerle images), 1602 (West-Zanen; Reijer Cornelisz), 1606 Matelief, 1607 Van der Hagen, 1611 Verken, 1616 Almeida, 1617 Van den Broecke (images), 1628 Altham, 1629 Herbert, 1629 Le Febvre, 1632 Wallesius, 1638 Mundy and Cauche, 1647 Verstegen (Japan), 1662 Arnhem survivors, then 1666–1691. The table excludes Rodrigues and Réunion.

## 2. The 1625 Wassenaer record (possibly new)

- **Source.** Nicolaes van Wassenaer, *'t Thiende deel of 't vervolgh van het Historisch verhael aller gedenck-waerdiger geschiedenissen ... van october des jaers 1625 tot april des jaers 1626* (Amsterdam: Jan Jansz., 1626), p. 121 and the following leaf, 121bis (Google pid PA121-IA1). Margin date "Februar. 1626".
  - Copies: Google Books `qZ5XDf72z2gC` (full view, public domain), also reached through Delpher boeken1. A second Delpher record, `aVrhrhSB-iYC` (catalogued 1622; a bound series), returns the same text.
  - Delpher: https://www.delpher.nl/nl/boeken1/gview?query=dodeersen&coll=boeken1&identifier=qZ5XDf72z2gC
- **Context (read from page images).**
  - p. 121: the Maeght van Dordrecht, the Leeuwinne and the jacht Wesop took their cargo at Gamron and Surat. They then "liepen op het Eylandt Mauritius, en bleven daer drie maenden". The Persian ambassador (Musa Beg, travelling to the Republic) "onthielt hem daer op 't landt".
  - p. 121bis: describes the island and its game, "men heefter in overvloet Bocken / Schiltpadden / Velthoenders / Dodeersen / en Duyven". The ambassador's servants went shooting inland every day and cooked the catch in his tent. The sailors ate so many "Velthoenderen en gevogelt" that they forgot the ship's food. A fish called "Steenbrasem" caused headaches; eels were good. The ships left with pigs from Mauritius, went on to the Cape and St Helena, and met a Spanish carrack. The English *Star* sailed in company.
- **Corroboration.**
  - DAS 5171.3 Dordrecht, 5172.1 Weesp and 5173.2 Leeuwin (departed 1625): "Stayed for 3 months at Mauritius". Weesp and Leeuwin were forced into Plymouth in Jan 1626.
  - GLOBALISE 1090_0226: Wesep, Dordrecht and the English "Sterre" had been at Mauritius.
- **Why it matters.** Table A1 has no record between Van den Broecke (1617, images only) and Altham (1628). This is a dated 1625 report naming both dodo ("Dodeersen") and red hen ("Velthoenders"). It is second-hand news writing, compiled from returning crews or letters, not an eyewitness journal. That should be stated plainly.
- **Literature check still needed.** Parish 2013; Cheke and Hume 2008; Winters, Hume and Leenstra 2017. Search terms: Wassenaer, Musa Beg, Leeuwinne, 1625. A web search found nothing.
- **Follow-up.** VOC Surat letters for 1625 (GLOBALISE inv. 1084–1090). Any journal or log of the English *Star* (BL IOR/L/MAR/A). Musa Beg embassy sources. Other Wassenaer parts for the 1628–29 return fleet and the 1632 Surat ships.

## 3. Accessible repositories and what they cover

### 3.1 Nationaal Archief, VOC archive 1.04.02 (outside the OBP series)

- **Full EAD inventory.** Downloadable, CC0: https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02/download/xml (16,548 file-level entries; 16,363 carry a digitization link).
- **No HTR text for these series.**
  - METS records (e.g. `https://service.archief.nl/gaf/api/mets/v1/<uuid>`) contain only image and IIIF groups.
  - Images are fetchable at reduced size through IIIF: `https://service.archief.nl/iip/iipsrv?IIIF=<path>.jp2/full/1600,/0/default.jpg`.
  - The NA's transcription search site (zoekintranscripties.nl) closed on 1 April 2025. Its HTR covered the OBP volumes, which GLOBALISE already includes.
  - The current NA search (zvt) has no transcription tab. No public full-text search reaches the journals below.
- **"Stukken betreffende de vroegste scheepstochten, 1602–1614"** (inv. 440–655). Digitized, image only, not in GLOBALISE.
  - **inv. 508:** journal of upper merchant Hendrick Jansz. Craen on the Gelderland, 13 Dec 1606 – 18 Jun 1610 (~106 images). DAS 5085: the Gelderland lay at Mauritius **2 Jul – 12 Nov 1609**, searching for the Erasmus. **Top manuscript lead.** No account from 1608–1610 is in Table A1.
  - **inv. 577:** letter of Hendrick van Boecholt (Witte Leeuw), left on Mauritius, 8 Aug 1610 (4 images).
  - **inv. 583:** notes of Claes Symonsz. van Venhuysen (Ceylon), 7 Jan – 6 Sep 1610 (8 images). The ship was at Mauritius.
  - **inv. 585:** letter of Dirk van der Eertbruggen, left on Mauritius, 8 Aug 1610 (4 images).
  - **inv. 586:** copy letters left on Mauritius by Steven Coteels and Cornelis Reyersz. (Ter Goes), 19–20 Aug 1610 (8 images, downloaded). The hand is difficult. A first look at scan 1 shows a voyage narrative (departure 30 Jan 1610, the Cape, etc.). It needs HTR or a palaeographer.
  - **inv. 587:** Coteels to Van der Eertbruggen on Mauritius, 15 Nov 1610 (2 images).
  - **inv. 458:** Gelderland journal (Craen), 1603–06, Van der Hagen fleet (~102 images). The fleet's homeward ships took on provisions at Mauritius for a month (DAS 5065, 5066). Table A1's "1607 Van der Hagen" (Begin ende Voortgangh) comes from the same fleet, so this manuscript is a parallel witness.
  - **inv. 449:** journal of Jan Jacob Rycx on the Hollandia, 1602–03, Van Warwijck fleet (~58 images).
  - **inv. 440:** note to the apothecaries and surgeons of the 14-ship fleet (1602) on collecting or drawing foreign plants and animals. Context for natural-history reporting, not a record.
- **"Scheepsjournalen" (Departement van de equipage).** Only four fall before 1660. All are digitized, image only.
  - **inv. 5049:** Bontekoe, Groningen, 1622–25 (~63 images). Covers the return in which Middelburg and Hollandia fell in with Réunion on 1 Apr 1625 (DAS 5170; GLOBALISE 1084_0570, 1089_0385). A Réunion lead; does not concern Mauritius.
  - **inv. 5050:** Hollandia, 1626, outward voyage under Schram (~40 images). No Mauritius call in DAS.
  - **inv. 5051:** Claes Bruyn's report on Lamey (Taiwan), 1633. Not relevant.
  - **inv. 5052:** Wezel, homeward 1637–38 under Artus Gijsels (68 pages). No Mauritius call is recorded in DAS.
  - The rest (5053–5127) date from 1671–1702. They are mostly outward journals to the Cape and Batavia; DAS lists Mauritius calls only for a few ships of that period.
- **Kamer Zeeland journals (inv. 11407–11439).** Mostly 18th century. inv. 11408 is the resolutions and day register of the Verenigde Provinciën, Batavia to the Republic, 1655–56; DAS records no Mauritius call.

### 3.2 Nationaal Archief, other collections (found by downloading the EADs)

- **1.11.01.01 (Aanwinsten).**
  - **inv. 734:** "Brief van H. van Bree, op het eiland Mauritius, aan Christiaan Huygens, secretaris van de Raad van State, 1606 januari 4". It falls during Matelief's stay (1–27 Jan 1606). Not digitized. A web search found no use of it in the dodo literature. **High-value on-site lead.**
  - **inv. 1137:** Griffioen journal, 27 Nov 1607 – 14 Feb 1610, Verhoeff fleet. Not digitized. Whether the ship called at Mauritius is unknown.
  - **inv. 429:** journal fragment of the Vlissingen (Kamer Zeeland), 1610–12. Not digitized; Mauritius call not established.
  - **inv. 115 and 119:** copy-books of instructions and journals, 1617–1681.
  - **inv. 121:** Tasman's journal, 1642–43 (digitized). Covers his stay of Sept–Oct 1642.
  - **inv. 112:** journal of Michiel Gerritsz. Boos, Maarsseveen and other ships, 1662–69 (digitized). The Maarsseveen sailed in the 1662 Vlamingh return fleet, the fleet of the Arnhem wreck.
- **1.10.30 (Geleynssen de Jongh).**
  - inv. 315: copy letters from the Nieuw-Haarlem in the South-East Harbour, May–Aug 1641. These are also in the OBP; the GLOBALISE copy has no fauna.
  - Wollebrant Geleynsz commanded the 's-Gravenhage, which stayed 27–28 days at Mauritius in 1632. That visit is the Wallesius voyage, so it is covered.

### 3.3 DAS (Dutch Asiatic Shipping)

- **Access.** https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/das. The "particulars" text field cannot be searched. Instead, the full results for departures 1595–1700 (2,900 voyages) were exported as `voyages_with_details.csv` and filtered for Mauritius, Mascarenhas, Rodrigues and Réunion: 128 rows, of which about 75 record actual stays at the island. The DAS rows are the backbone of the target list in §5.

### 3.4 DBNL (full text)

- **Coverage.** Searched for 30 dodo spellings (rate-limited, 12 s between requests). DBNL holds few 17th-century voyage texts and **no Linschoten-Vereeniging editions** were hit.
- **Hits:**
  - Coolhaas (ed.), "Een bron van het historische gedeelte van Hugo de Groots De Jure Praedae", *BMHG* 79 (1965), ~pp. 466–471. Contains the 1601 Wolfert Harmensz fleet entries ("dodoerssen" on Rodrigues, 23 Sept 1601; "dronten ofte dodersen" on Mauritius, Sept–Oct 1601) and a castaway found on Mauritius. **Known** through Hume 2003 and Moree 2001. Coolhaas's edition of this text is not cited in Table A1, but the voyage is covered there. https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bij005196401_01/_bij005196401_01_0008.php
  - Raven-Hart, *Cape Good Hope 1652–1702*: "Dodersen" used there for penguins (Cape, 6 Apr 1654). A naming point, not a dodo record.
  - Adriaen van de Venne, *Tafereel van de belacchende werelt* (1635): an image caption, "WALGH-VOGEL ... à nautis DODAERS". Cultural reference.
  - P. Nylandt, *Schouw-toneel der aertsche schepselen* (1668): "Dod-aersen, of Dronten". Compilation.
  - Bidloo, *De muitery en nederlaag van Midas* (1690s): "Dodeers" as a character name. Cultural reference.
  - *Vaderlandsche Letteroefeningen* 1779: Morel's essay in translation.
  - The rest are modern ("dodaars" is the little grebe) or noise.

### 3.5 Delpher (books, Google-scanned books, periodicals)

- **Access.** The search UI is JavaScript-driven; its own JSON endpoint (`/nl/pres/results/multi?actions[]=results&query=…&coll=boeken|boeken1|dts`) returns results and was used politely. No login or bot challenge was involved. Searched for 27 spellings across 3 collections, plus "Mauritius" with Velthoenders and vogels.
- **Primary or early-printed hits:**
  - Wassenaer 1626, Dodeersen and Velthoenders (§2).
  - Heidfeld, *Sphinx theologico-philosophica* (Dutch ed., 1627), p. 88, marginal heading "Getrouheyt vande Indische Veldhoenderen". It reads "De Hollanders comende op 't Eylant van Mauritius, vonden eenighe Velthoenders ...": a caught bird's cries draw the others, which are then killed. The behaviour matches later red-hen accounts (Herbert 1634; Wallesius 1632). Its source is unidentified. It may be a pre-1627 printed transmission of an early Dutch report; it is not an eyewitness account.
  - *Copye van eenen brief uyt Zuratten* (1633; Google `iPhGAAAAcAAJ`). Known: Winters, Hume and Leenstra 2017. Its mock glossary equates "Borghemeesters" with "Dottaersen" and "Soldaten" with "Velthoenders".
  - Commelin, *Begin ende Voortgangh* (1645–46), index entries "Walch-vogels" and "Dodaersen ofte Dronten". Known.
  - Matelief, *Journael* (1648). Known.
  - Van Twist (1638), index "Walch-Vogels". This is the bound Commelin material; known.
  - Nieuhof, *Gezantschap* (1665/1670): velthoenders and tortoises at Mauritius. Compilation.
  - Gerrit van Spaan, *De gelukzoeker over zee* (1694): Mauritius birds caught by hand. Late and semi-fictional.
  - The rest are 18th-century natural history (Buffon translations, Linnaeus) or OCR noise for "doders" and "doctoers".
- **Periodicals (dts).** No primary voyage material before 1800 beyond the above.
- **Newspapers 1618–1799.** Already negative (previous session).

### 3.6 Internet Archive full-text search

- **Access.** `https://be-api.us.archive.org/ia-pub-fts-api?q=…&size=50&from=…`. Searched for 22 spellings, about 1,000 hits.
- **Hits.** Almost all are 19th- and 20th-century secondary works: Strickland 1848, Hamel 1848, Owen, Pitot 1905, newspapers. Early primary hits: Verken 1613 (*Eylffter Schiffart*, "Totersten oder Walckvögel"; known) and Nieuhof 1682 (compilation).
- **Linschoten-Vereeniging.** The Society's site says its volumes are on IA, but IA metadata turns up only the 1910 *Itinerario*. Most LV editions from after 1925 (Keuning's *Tweede Schipvaart*, Coolhaas's Van den Broecke, Hoogewerff's Bontekoe and others) are not freely searchable. The LV "werken" page now returns 404.

### 3.7 Not reached or not verified

- **Zeeuws Archief.** Most of the Zeeland VOC chamber archive is in NA 1.04.02.
- **Other holders not checked online.** The Westfries Archief, the Scheepvaartmuseum, the Leiden UB BPL manuscripts and the KB manuscripts were not checked. Their journal holdings for 1598–1660 need a catalogue search; none was verified in this session.
- **Google Books.** It blocked the text-mode endpoint after a few requests (automated-query block). No attempt was made to get round it. Page images reached through normal search-result links loaded.

## 4. GLOBALISE: voyages documented at the Mascarenes, 1598–1660

- **Method.** All GLOBALISE pages matching Mauritius spellings in volumes catalogued up to 1660: 2,539 windows. Windows about Fort Mauritius on Makian, ships named Mauritius, or Prince Maurits were removed; 1,249 scans remained. Four model readers extracted the voyages (`journals/visits0–3.json`). Machine output, not checked against scans.
- **Dodo or red-hen terms found.** Only 1059_0051 and 0277–0278 (1615, known to this pilot) and 1095_0922 (1629 Nassau, known). No new dodo term turned up.
- **Fauna incidental to the target list.**
  - 1143_0618 (1642): the island is "vol van vee en alderhande vogel".
  - 1239_0143 (1662): a "pijlstaert" (tropicbird).
  - 3993_0152 (1658): cattle near the fort.

## 6. Ranked next steps

1. **Wassenaer 1625.** Read p. 121 and 121bis in full, transcribe and date it. Check Parish 2013, Cheke and Hume 2008, Hume 2006 and Winters et al. 2017 for "Wassenaer" or "1625". Then search the other Wassenaer parts (1622–1632) for "Mauritius", especially those covering the 1628–29 return fleet and the 1632 Surat ships.
2. **NA 1.04.02 inv. 508 (Gelderland, Craen).** Locate the July–November 1609 Mauritius section (~106 images) and read it, by HTR (a Transkribus or Loghi model trained on VOC hands) or by eye. It records a four-month stay, and Table A1 has nothing between 1607 and 1611.
3. **NA 1.04.02 inv. 577 and 583–587.** Letters and notes left on Mauritius by the Ceylon, Ter Goes and Witte Leeuw crews, 1610: 26 images in all. Small enough to transcribe in full.
4. **NA 1.11.01.01 inv. 734.** H. van Bree's letter from Mauritius, 4 Jan 1606. On-site request, or a scan order.
5. **Van Warwijck's return, 1606** (Hollandia and Dordrecht, 7 months), and **Erasmus, 1608–09** (about a year). Look for journals in NA 1.04.02 inv. 440–472 and in Commelin's account of the 1602 fleet. Also check Keuning's or Moree's editions for the Hollandia.
6. **Coen 1612** (Colenbrander, *Bescheiden* I) and **Van der Zande 1613**. Printed editions; read the Mauritius letters.
7. **English *Star*, 1625**, and English wintering ships 1627–1657. Check BL IOR/L/MAR/A logs and Foster's calendars (earlier probe negative for "dodo"; re-check with "hens" and "Mauritius").
8. **Heidfeld 1627.** Identify the source of the "Velthoenders" passage (the German or Latin *Sphinx* editions, or a Dutch interpolation) before claiming anything.
9. **HTR for image-only NA series.** Run a VOC-trained HTR model over the digitized early-voyage series (inv. 440–655) and journals inv. 5049–5052. Then run the same lexical dodo sweep used on GLOBALISE.
10. **Catalogues not checked.** Westfries Archief (Hoorn and Enkhuizen chambers), Het Scheepvaartmuseum, Leiden UB (BPL) and KB manuscript catalogues: search for journals of the ships in §5, especially items 4, 8, 9, 13, 14, 21, 25 and 28.

**Update 1 Oct 2026 (user check):** Wassenaer's 1625 Mauritius passage is **known**. Parish (2013) quotes it in full in his section "The Amsterdam Dodo of 1626", as the source for the live dodo that reached Holland in 1626 on the Dordrecht, Weesp or Leeuwin. Cheke & Hume (2008) have no 1625 entry.
