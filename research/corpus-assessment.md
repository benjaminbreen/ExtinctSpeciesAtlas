# First-corpus assessment · September 2026

This is a research triage, not a claim that the twelve dossiers are complete. “Human attribution” asks whether human action materially contributed to extinction, not whether one particular mechanism can be proved as the sole cause. “Textual opportunity” asks whether digitized written material could be found, aligned, translated, or reattributed with language-model assistance. Every proposed addition still needs a passage-level check against an edition or archival object.

| Silphium | **Unresolved**: the ancient Cyrenaic plant's biological identity and extinction status remain open; overgrazing, harvesting and environmental change have all been proposed. | **High for corpus-building; low for proving a survival date.** | Align Greek, Latin, later medical and Arabic plant names with place, product, and context. A late use of the name may denote a substitute resin. [Updated review](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-9408/5/2/51); [source dossier](../silphium.html). |
| Aurochs | **Well supported**: hunting and habitat contraction preceded the reported 1627 death of the last wild animal in Poland. | **Medium–high.** | Join classical descriptions to early modern German, Latin, and Polish inventories, protection orders, and reports. Distinguish a living wild aurochs from domestic cattle or bison in later texts. [Population-history study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2858146/); [Herberstein research](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/nanna/record/7510/files/Kitchen_uchicago_0330D_17030.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0). |
| Dodo | **Well supported**: settlement, hunting, and introduced animals. | **Medium.** | Reconcile Dutch voyage reports and drawings with later taxonomic readings. The famous archive is already heavily studied; careful source correction is more likely than a dramatic new last sighting. [Natural History Museum](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/the-dodo-bird-the-real-facts-about-this-icon-of-extinction.html). |
| Rodrigues solitaire | **Well supported**: human arrival and introduced species. | **High.** | Compare [Leguat's 1708 voyage](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/11437565.pdf) with later French visitors and the nineteenth-century debate over whether the bird was real. [Taxonomic review](https://academic.oup.com/zoolinnean/article/doi/10.1093/zoolinnean/zlae086/7733394). |
| Steller's sea cow | **Well supported**: rapid hunting on fur-trade voyages after Steller's observation. | **Medium.** | Check Steller's text against Russian voyage and provisioning records; separate observations from later reconstructions. | Rodrigues saddle-backed tortoise | **Well supported**: large-scale removal and introduced species. | **High, paired with the domed tortoise.** | Search shipping, food-supply and island records; identify when “tortoises” can and cannot be assigned to *C. vosmaeri*. [IUCN specialist-group review](https://iucn-tftsg.org/wp-content/uploads/file/Accounts/crm_5_000e_fossil_checklist_v1_2015.pdf). |
| Rodrigues domed tortoise | **Well supported**: same exploitation history. | **High, paired with the saddle-backed tortoise.** | The key problem is species attribution inside a corpus that often does not distinguish shell form. [Morphological and ancient-DNA study](https://iucn-tftsg.org/wp-content/uploads/file/Articles/Austin_and_Arnold_2001.pdf). |
| Bluebuck | **Strongly implicated; exact trajectory less settled.** Hunting and habitat change met an already small or declining range. | **Very high.** | Compare German, Dutch, French and English descriptions with the small and sometimes misidentified museum specimen set; reconstruct dates and places of reports. [Specimen and historical-record analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7822880/); [paleogenomic study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9750129/). |
| Mauritius blue pigeon | **Well supported**: deforestation and introduced predators. | **High.** | Trace early Dutch drawings, French descriptions, Sonnerat's specimens and nineteenth-century last reports. [Natural History Museum](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2020/november/invasive-species-impact-on-island-flora-and-fauna.html); [Sonnerat plate](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb44555801p). |
| Great auk | **Well supported**: commercial exploitation followed by specimen and egg collecting. | **Medium.** | A large multilingual fishing and natural-history record remains to index, although the central extinction narrative is well known. [Natural History Museum](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/worlds-collide-great-auk.html). |
| Cuban macaw | **Strongly implicated; exact mix of causes unsettled.** Historical sources document killing and trade; reports of the last birds disagree. | **Very high.** | Search Spanish local accounts and collection histories, then separate first-hand sightings from retrospective dates and from reports of other macaws. [Wiley and Kirwan's review, especially pp. 133–140](https://boc-online.org/bulletins/downloads/BBOC1332-PDFa.pdf). |
| Falkland Islands wolf | **Well supported**: persecution by settlers and poisoning as sheep farming expanded. | **Medium.** | Join Darwin's account to earlier voyages and nineteenth-century settlement records; keep the chronology of last reports distinct from causes. ## Practical order for the next dossiers

1. **Bluebuck and Cuban macaw**: the best chance of useful new source-level findings, with multilingual texts, competing dates, and museum objects to cross-check.
2. **Mauritius blue pigeon and the two Rodrigues tortoises**: compact island archives where names, illustrations, specimens and shipping records can be systematically joined. Treat the tortoises as two taxa sharing one research dossier.
3. **Rodrigues solitaire and aurochs**: strong primary texts plus tractable French or central-European administrative material.
4. **Silphium**: continue the broad source search, but keep name continuity separate from biological continuity.
5. **Great auk, dodo, Steller's sea cow, and warrah**: historically valuable comparisons with firmer causal accounts; prioritize a source inventory and gaps in existing bibliographies over a search for a sensational last date.

Language models are most useful here for generating candidate references, variant names, OCR corrections, aligned translations, and contradictions to investigate. A proposed new appearance date should enter the atlas only with its original passage, edition or object, provenance, and an explicit identification argument.
