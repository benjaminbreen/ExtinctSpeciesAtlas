Original text

pp. 24–25 · description

A. with sharp-pointed, taper, arcuated horns bending backwards, marked with twenty prominent rings, but smooth towards their points, twenty inches long: ears sharp-pointed, above nine inches in length: larger than a buck: color, when alive, a fine blue; when dead, changes to a bluish grey, with a mixture of white: hairs long: beneath each eye a large white mark: belly white: tail seven inches long; the hairs at the end six inches. From a skin bought at Amsterdam1, brought from the Cape of Good Hope; where they are found, but far up the country. This is the species which, from the length of its hair and form of the horns, connects this genus with that of the Goat.

Note

    Pennant describes a preserved skin. His statement about the colour of the living animal is reported information, rather than a field observation. The heading is “Blue”; “Blue Goat” appears among the citations, referring to Kolben’s Cape, II, p. 114. ↩
