Original text

II, p. 47 · extract

I observed, in the former volume, that in the neighbourhood of this river was once to be found the Leucophæa or blue antelope, but that, for many years past, it had been lost to the colony. I understood, however1, that, a few months before we evacuated the Cape, a small herd of this beautiful animal had again made its appearance among the wooded hills behind Sweet Milk Valley, where, instead of suffering them to remain unmolested, at least for some time, the farmers were lying in wait for their destruction.

Note

  1. 01

    Barrow names no informant and gives no anatomical description of the reported herd. “A few months before we evacuated the Cape” suggests late 1802 or early 1803, before the British evacuation in 1803; this date is inferred. The report does not independently confirm that bluebuck survived beyond c. 1800. The same account reappears in the second edition (1806), vol. I, pp. 371–372. ↩
