  1. 01

    Herodotus

    c. 484–c. 425 BCE

    History · Greek

    Historian from Halicarnassus. Book 4 of the Histories places the silphium country among the peoples of Libya.

    Image ↗ Roman copy of a Greek bust, second century CE. Metropolitan Museum of Art · CC0.

    01 passage
    • Histories 4.169 ↗
  2. 02

    Hippocratic corpus

    late 5th–4th c. BCE

    Medicine · Greek · composite authorship

    A collection of medical treatises by multiple authors. Diseases IV discusses attempts to cultivate Libyan silphium in Ionia and the Peloponnese.

    Image ↗ Later engraving of Hippocrates, the corpus’s traditional namesake; the author of Diseases IV is unknown. Unidentified engraver · public domain.

    01 passage
    • Diseases IV.34 ↗
  3. 03

    Theophrastus

    c. 371–c. 287 BCE

    Botany · Greek

    Greek philosopher and naturalist. Enquiry into Plants records the form, harvesting, range, and reported cultivation of silphium.

    Image ↗ Modern statue at Palermo Botanical Garden. tato grasso / Singinglemon · CC BY-SA 2.5.

    01 passage
    • Enquiry into Plants 6.3 ↗
  4. 04

    Plautus

    c. 254–184 BCE

    Comedy · Latin

    Roman comic playwright. In Rudens, a character invokes a hoped-for silphium harvest and a shipment to Capua.

    Image ↗ Imagined portrait engraved in 1770. Pierre François Barrois · public domain.

    01 passage
    • Rudens 630–31 ↗
  5. 05

    Catullus

    c. 84–c. 54 BCE

    Poetry · Latin

    Roman poet of the late Republic. Poem 7 uses silphium-bearing Cyrene as a point of comparison.

    Image ↗ Twentieth-century bust at Sirmione. Schorle · CC BY-SA 3.0.

    01 passage
    • Poem 7 ↗
  6. 06

    Strabo

    64/63 BCE–c. 24 CE

    Geography · Greek

    Greek geographer from Amaseia. Geography 17 describes a long, narrow silphium-producing region south of Cyrenaica.

    Image ↗ Imagined portrait engraved by André Thévet in 1584. André Thévet · public domain.

    01 passage
    • Geography 17.3.23 ↗
  7. 07

    Pliny the Elder

    23/24–79 CE

    Natural history · Latin

    Roman encyclopedist. Two passages in Natural History discuss scarcity of Cyrenaic silphium, imported resin, and medicinal uses.

    Image ↗ Nineteenth-century imagined portrait, published in 1859. Geoffrey · public domain.

    02 passages
    • Natural History 19.38–42 ↗
    • Natural History 22.100–101 ↗
  8. 08

    Dioscorides

    fl. c. 50–70 CE

    Medicine · Greek

    Greek physician and pharmacological author. De materia medica 3.80 compares silphium and resin from Libya and eastern regions.

    Image ↗ Dioscorides depicted in the Vienna Dioscurides manuscript, c. 512 CE. Unknown artist · public domain.

    01 passage
    • De materia medica 3.80 ↗

    Martin Martin

    fl. 1690s–1719

    Travel writing · Gaelic / English

    Traveller from Skye who visited St Kilda in 1697. His 1698 book describes the Gairfowl’s body, egg, and seasonal presence.

    Image ↗ Typographic initials; no portrait used. Atlas of Historically Lost Species · original asset.

  15. 15

    Georg Wilhelm Steller

    1709–1746

    Natural history · Bering’s second Kamchatka expedition

    German physician and naturalist in Russian service. Shipwrecked with Bering’s crew on Bering Island in 1741–1742, where he described the sea cow from living and dissected animals.

    Image ↗ Typographic initials; no portrait used. Atlas of Historically Lost Species · original asset.

    02 passages
    • De bestiis marinis (1751), herds and feeding ↗
    • De bestiis marinis (1751), capture ↗

    John Barrow

    1764–1848

    Travel writing · British colonial administration

    Travelled in southern Africa while serving under Lord Macartney at the Cape. His account includes a reported reappearance of blue antelope near Sweet Milk Valley.

    Image ↗ Typographic initials; no portrait used. Atlas of Historically Lost Species · original asset.

    01 passage
    • An Account of Travels into the Interior of Southern Africa, II, p. 47 ↗
  19. 19

    Martin Sauer

    fl. 1785–1802

    Expedition account · Russian service

    English secretary to Joseph Billings’s Russian expedition to north-eastern Siberia and the Aleutian Islands, 1785–1794. His published account reports the sea cow’s extinction at second hand.

    Image ↗ Typographic initials; no portrait used. Atlas of Historically Lost Species · original asset.

    01 passage
    • An Account of a Geographical and Astronomical Expedition (1802), p. 181 ↗

    Alfred Newton

    Image ↗ Engraved portrait in The Graphic, 1887. The Graphic / Historian 1875 · public domain / CC0.

    • Wolley’s researches, Ibis (1861) ↗

    A. E. Nordenskiöld

    1832–1901

    Arctic exploration · geology

    Finnish-born Swedish geologist and explorer. Led the Vega expedition through the Northeast Passage, 1878–1880, and gathered testimony about the sea cow on Bering Island in 1879.

    Image ↗ Typographic initials; no portrait used. Atlas of Historically Lost Species · original asset.

    01 passage
    • The Voyage of the Vega (1881), II, pp. 276–278 ↗
