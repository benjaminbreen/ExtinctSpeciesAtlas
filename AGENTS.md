# Editorial and development notes

This site is a research atlas by a historian of science and medicine. Its subject is species with written historical testimony that disappeared before 1900. Fossil-only or Paleolithic cases are outside its scope. Cases with disputed taxonomic identity or extinction status must say so plainly.

Write in a direct academic voice that remains readable to a general audience. Do not add promotional copy, slogans, aphorisms, sentimental lines about absence or memory, generic “storytelling” prose, or decorative text with no evidentiary purpose. Do not invent quotations or make a source seem more conclusive than it is.

Public-facing copy should use straightforward bibliographic information and compact labels: for example, “pp. 390–392,” not “Transcription reproduced from the existing atlas record. The facing scan shows p. 391 only; the passage spans pp. 390–392.” Use the author and actual work title rather than invented editorial headings. Do not add AI-written framing about what a narrative “allows us to distinguish,” how readers should evaluate evidence, or why the research matters unless explicitly requested. Keep agent workflow, verification status reports, production requirements, and research-procedure commentary in the conversation or internal research records, not on the public site. These instructions do not remove source citations, image credits, translation attribution, or specific factual uncertainty required to avoid a misleading claim.

For each historical claim, prefer a specific primary-source passage and a link to an edition. Attribute translations. Keep interpretation distinct from what the source directly reports. Label approximate geography and uncertain identifications. Date and credit historical images, and distinguish depictions made long after the species from observations made while it existed.

Edited records live in `data/passages.json`, species entries in `data/species.json`, and further reading in `data/bibliography.json`. Run `python3 scripts/build_site.py` to build the site and `python3 scripts/build_site.py --check` to check it. Edit templates/renderers instead of generated pages. The silphium source records are generated by `scripts/build_sources.py`; the great auk records by `scripts/build_auk_sources.py`. Both are committed as standalone HTML. When adding or changing historical text, check the transcription against its linked edition, label omissions, and review working translations before publication. A layout-only build must preserve the existing text and does not imply a new source review. Further reading should identify works relevant to the particular primary source, with enough bibliographic detail to find them. Keep place links and subject filters tied to explicit entry data; distinguish a growing region from a trade destination or contextual association.

Add interpretive prose only when requested by Benjamin. Preserve existing approved notes; a necessary factual qualification should be concise and linked to the relevant passage. Do not create sections of routine cautions. The Authorities index is generated by `scripts/build_authorities.py` from `data/authorities.json` and the work links in `data/passages.json`; keep it synchronized with both source indexes and the author links on source pages. Record an approximate birth/death or floruit date, distinguish composite or uncertain authorship, and label portrait images as later representations. Preserve the Wikimedia source URL, attribution, and license in `assets/authorities/manifest.json`.

The design should remain minimalist and responsive, with clear reading order and restrained interaction. Keep the species index easy to scan and the source and map controls usable by keyboard.

The homepage's species figures are generated interface illustrations. Keep them visibly and separately labeled as such; never place them in a historical image gallery or cite them as evidence for a species' appearance. Preserve the contact-sheet mapping, prompts, and historical visual points of departure in `assets/species-icons/`.

The small colored seal uses original SVG specimen-detail marks in `assets/species-icons/seal-marks.js`. Treat them as navigational graphics rather than historical source images. Keep the dark woodcut-style ink, carved highlights, and species-specific accent colors; do not substitute generic white line icons or present the marks as facsimiles. On the homepage, the vertical hairline is a page element between the circle and the mark in depth. It stays with the hero when the seal follows scrolling. Keep the hero image caption bibliographic; place image interpretation in its linked note.

The silphium reference panel and gallery are attributed snapshots of external resources, checked on the dates stated in the README. When refreshing them, verify what each platform actually claims, preserve file-level image credits and license links, and never equate a reused plant name with the ancient Cyrenaic plant. Reports of a stalk, a garden plant, and a traded resin are different kinds of evidence; a late mention of “silphium” is not automatically a verified survival date. The source index is explicitly in progress, so extend it with verified passage references rather than implying it is already exhaustive.
