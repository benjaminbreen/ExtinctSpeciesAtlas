The record

Five texts, from St Kilda in 1698 to the Funk Island expedition of 1887.

Martin Martin St Kilda

01

“The sea-fowls are, first, Gairfowl, being the stateliest, as well as the largest of all the fowls here, and above the size of a solan goose, of a black colour, red about the eyes, a large white spot under each eye, a long broad bill; stands stately, its whole body erected, its wings short, it flyeth not at all …”

A Late Voyage to St Kilda, 1698, chapter II

Reported placeSt Kilda ↗, Outer Hebrides
SubjectForm, egg, flight, and season
Text / languageA Late Voyage to St Kilda · English

Martin’s account gives the earliest of the five detailed great auk passages here.

Pennant British Zoology

02

“According to Mr. Martin, this bird breeds on the isle of St. Kilda, appearing there the beginning of May, and retiring the middle of June. It lays one egg, which is six inches long, of a white color; some are irregularly marked with purplish lines crossing each other, others blotched with black and ferruginous about the thicker end.”

Volume II, pp. 507–508 · spelling regularized

Reported placeSt Kilda ↗, Outer Hebrides
SubjectBreeding season, egg, body, and wings
Text / languageBritish Zoology, fourth edition · English

Pennant combines Martin’s report with further descriptions of the bird’s body and wings.

Cartwright Journal

03

“Funk Island is a small flat island-rock, about twenty leagues east of the island of Fogo, in the latitude of 50° north. Innumerable flocks of sea-fowl breed upon it every summer, which are of great service to the poor inhabitants of Fogo; who make voyages there to load with birds and eggs.”

Entry of 5 July 1785 · published 1792 · spelling regularized

Harvesting placeFunk Island ↗, off Newfoundland
Recorded usesFood, eggs, and feathers
Text / languageJournal, volume III, p. 55 · English

Cartwright describes boatloads of birds and the practice of collecting their feathers.

Newton Ibis

04

“The party consisted of fourteen men: two of these are dead, but with all the remaining twelve we conversed. They were commanded, as I have just said, by Vilhjalmur, and started in an eight-oared boat from Kyrkjuvogr, one evening between the 2nd and 5th of June, 1844. The next morning early they arrived off Eldey.”

Newton, 1861, pp. 390–391 · transcription

Capture placeEldey ↗, southwest Iceland
TestimonyNewton and Wolley interviewed twelve members of the party
Text / languageIbis 3 (1861): 390–392 · English

Newton reconstructs the 1844 capture from interviews conducted in Iceland.

Lucas Funk Island

05

“A large portion of the southern and most extensive swell of rock is thickly covered with vegetation, this, the former breeding ground of the Great Auk, being mapped out in vivid green by the plants nourished by the decomposed bodies and slowly decomposing bones of the long extinct bird. It would seem that the Auk inhabited every accessible foot of ground, the inability of the bird to fly restricting it of necessity to such portions of the island as could be reached after a landing had been effected on the northerly or southerly slope.”

Smithsonian report for 1888, p. 507 · transcription

Field siteFunk Island ↗, off Newfoundland
EvidenceTerrain, vegetation, and great auk bones
Text / languageNational Museum Report for 1888 · English

Lucas visited the former breeding ground in July 1887 and mapped its surviving remains.

01 / SummaryWikipedia ↗

The great auk was a flightless North Atlantic seabird. In 1844, two birds were captured on Eldey, Iceland.
