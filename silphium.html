Pliny Natural History 19

02

“For many years now it has not been found in that country. In our own time only one stalk was found and sent to the emperor Nero.”

Translation for this atlas from the Latin · 19.39

Growing regionCyrenaica ↗
Trade & substitutesRome ↗; Persian, Median, and Armenian resin
Text / languageNatural History 19.38–42 · Latin

Pliny reports scarcity of the Cyrenaic plant. He also says resin from other regions was still available, so his account requires attention to which plant and product each name denotes.

Synesius Letter 106

03

“I asked the young man about the silphium: had it come from your own cultivation, or had you received it as a gift?”

Translation for this atlas from the Greek

Growing placeHis brother’s garden; town not named in this letter
Regional contextCyrenaica ↗; inferred from the correspondence
Text / languageLetter 106 · Greek

A plant called silphium was being grown and exchanged. The letter does not establish whether it was botanically identical to the prized plant described by earlier authors.

03 / Dated reportsPrimary texts

Later appearances

  1. 54–68 CE
    Pliny, Natural History 19.39 ↗A single Cyrenaic stalk reportedly sent to Nero during his reign.
  2. 402 CE
    Synesius, Letter 106 ↗A plant called silphium sent from his brother’s garden.
  3. 405 CE
    Synesius, Letter 134 ↗Silphium juice listed among gifts from Cyrene.

Dates refer to a reported plant, a named product, or a text—not a verified last living specimen of one identified species. Letter 134 reports juice, not a plant in situ.

Visual record

Images

Two seventeenth-century woodcuts and the twelve files currently in Wikimedia Commons’ Cyrene silphium coin category ↗. The woodcuts are later identifications; the coins are ancient objects photographed or reproduced much later.

The 1644 edition calls the first plant laserpitium from van Stapel’s garden. Neither woodcut verifies the appearance of ancient Cyrenaic silphium.
